# Return (Dan Harmon's Story Circle, step 7)

**Source:** Harmon:

- SS103;
- SS104, section "7. 'Return' - Bringing It Home," and the 3/7 symmetry in section 5;
- SS105, the TV version;
- SS106, entry 7;
- Harmon blog, 18 Feb. 2014.

Also the StudioBinder Workbook (step 7) and Worksheet. Short citations follow the key in `../01 - Overview and principles.md`.

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is
At step 7 the protagonist **goes back to the familiar situation** they started from (SS101; SS104 list). Harmon's short rationale is that a journey that never comes back isn't a journey (SS106). SS103 explains why this matters to him most of all: humans are a community, and a hero who stayed up the beanstalk forever would have done the tribe no good.

On the circle, step 7 is the **return threshold**. It is the second crossing of the horizontal line, now upward, out of chaos and the unconscious and back into order and the conscious, ordinary world. It mirrors the downward crossing at step 3 (SS104).

## How it works
- **Easy returns and hard ones** (SS104):
  - For some characters the return is effortless, as simple as saying goodbye to the scarecrow and waking up (*The Wizard of Oz*).
  - For others, outside help finally arrives to extract them. This is Campbell's **Rescue from Without**.
  - For others it is a struggle, which is why Campbell also describes **the Magic Flight**.
  - In the flat-tire anecdote, the return is simply the man climbing back into his car.
- **Why returns are contested.** The inhabitants of each world want the two kept apart.
  - The people upstairs never wanted you going down.
  - The creatures of the deep don't want people strolling back out, least of all ones who have seen the depths and lived to tell of it.
  - Whatever their stated motives, both sides work to keep the worlds separate (SS104).
- **Natural set pieces** (SS104):
  - Harmon calls this an ideal spot for a car chase.
  - In a love story, the hero, now knowing what matters, bursts out onto the street because the beloved's plane leaves in minutes.
  - McClane, who feared flying at step 1, lashes himself to a fire hose, leaps from the exploding roof, and shoots out a window to swing back inside.
  - Pointedly, he soon ends up back in the room where the Christmas party began.
- **Quick examples** (SS106): a pursuit by car; a large-scale rescue; arriving home with a flower for a partner; a jump from an exploding tower.
- **The chase and rescue stages are optional** (Harmon blog, 18 Feb. 2014).
  - A reader thought the circle fit Campbell well until steps 6 and 7. Harmon replied that he doesn't read Campbell as saying every story has a magic flight and a rescue from without.
  - Campbell, in his view, was cataloging recurring ways that heroes get home before turning to the more basic idea: the return threshold itself.
  - Harmon says his tutorials meant the same: however it happens, the hero comes back.
- **Things may follow the hero home.** This belongs to step 8 in Harmon's text, but it begins with how rough the crossing at 7 was.
  - When the return is messy, more chaos than intended can slip through the doorway.
  - The villain may follow the hero up into the ordinary world (SS104, step 8).
- **What the StudioBinder Workbook says.**
  - Step 7 is a false resolution or exposition sequence, leading to a twist: the audience has been set up to expect one thing, and it turns around.
  - Its *Dark Knight* example: the protagonist returns changed. Bruce's need to retire Batman died with Rachel, he must now protect Gotham, and the Joker has corrupted Harvey, making Batman's last task more painful (SB Workbook, step 7).
  - ⚠️ "False resolution" and "twist" are sequence-approach terms, not Harmon's. Harmon's step 7 is the escape and crossing back.
- **TV version.** The sitcom version: the consequences of step 6 must be undone (SS105).

## Placement
- **On the circle:** the nine o'clock point, where the horizontal line meets the left edge of the circle, on the boundary between the lower and upper halves (SS101; SS106).
- **In time:** at the end of the third quarter, around 225 seconds into a five-minute pilot. The third quarter ends with starting to head back toward the original circumstances (SS106). ⚠️ About 75%, by our arithmetic.
- Harmon gives no Syd Field term for step 7. He maps Plot Point 2 to step 6 (SS106).

## Equivalents in other frameworks
- **Campbell:** Magic Flight, Rescue from Without, and Crossing of the Return Threshold (SS104 text; SS106; the SS104 diagram labels the point "return threshold").
  - Refusal of the Return is not placed. ⚠️ Harmon's step-5 discussion of the temptation to stay overlaps it.
- **Snyder:** ⚠️ Break into Three (p. 85) (Snyder, ch. 4 "Let's Beat It Out!").
  - Snyder's Act 3 is the "synthesis" world, a fusion of the old world and its upside-down version. It is the closest analogue to re-entering the ordinary world changed.
  - Snyder's Break into Three is a moment of insight, where A and B stories join to produce the solution. Harmon's Return is more often physical: an escape or rescue.
  - The physical action of Harmon's step 7 may fall partly inside Snyder's Finale. ⚠️
- **Truby:** ⚠️ step 17, Third revelation and decision, and step 18, Gate, gauntlet, visit to death (both PDF p. 131). In Truby the pressure narrows and the hero passes a gate or gauntlet just before the battle, which resembles Harmon's chase and leap.
- **Bell:** ⚠️ Doorway of No Return #2, given in the composite master as Bell's name for Break into Three.
- **Weiland:** ⚠️ the Third Plot Point. Note that the master equates her Third Plot Point with All Is Lost, which would put it nearer Harmon's step 6. The Weiland mapping is uncertain.
- **Sequence approach:** ⚠️ the false resolution and twist (StudioBinder's vocabulary).

## In the composite
The master (`story-structure-definitions.md`) names "RETURN" only in its standing-rule list. It does not define it.

The nearest defined beat is **Break into Three** (Snyder / Bell "Doorway of No Return #2"). The gap analysis records Campbell's Crossing the Return Threshold as an alias of Break into Three.

Campbell's **Magic Flight**, which Harmon places at this step, is one of the beats the master **deliberately excludes**. Rescue from Without is not defined in the master.
