## What it is
Step 4 is the protagonist **adapting** to the unfamiliar situation (SS101): trying things, getting organized, and being broken down (SS106). Harmon prefers Campbell's name for it, **the Road of Trials**. He prefers it to Vogler's "friends, enemies and allies" and to the "training phase" that he says hack producers call it, because Campbell's term is less specific (SS104). The core idea is sink or swim: the protagonist has been pitched into deep water (SS104).

The deeper function is **stripping away**.
- Harmon points out that Campbell likens this stage to digestion: the hero is broken down and rid of neuroses, fear and desire.
- The unconscious "basement" has no room for the props of everyday life. Inhalers, glasses, credit cards, promotions and phones can't help there.
- The hero's purpose becomes refreshingly, and frighteningly, simple (SS104).
- The story is heading toward the lowest depths of the mind, and the hero can't get there carrying everything he used to think mattered (SS104).

## How it works
- **Don't take "training" literally.** Harmon complains about films that waste screen time on a hero doing literal exercises in the woods, just because someone assumed such a scene was required. The point is survival in the new element, not a montage (SS104).
- **Examples of shedding** (SS104):
  - In *Romancing the Stone*, Douglas's character hacks the heels off the heroine's pricey shoes and tosses her luggage over a precipice. To survive the jungle she has to drop her baggage, literally.
  - In *Die Hard*, McClane is told by a terrorist he had earlier spared not to hesitate next time. McClane then kills him and thanks the body for the advice. The policeman is peeling away to reveal the cowboy underneath.
  - In the flat-tire anecdote, the man opens a trunk full of laundry and fast-food trash, shuffles it around, then loses patience and starts flinging it onto the roadside.
- **More examples** (SS106): a detective interviews suspects; a cowboy rounds up riders; a popular girl gives a nerd a shopping makeover.
- **Allies and equipment gathered here pay off at step 8.**
  - Step 4 faces step 8 across the circle. What the hero picks up here is what lets him triumph at the end:
    - a lighter a tramp gave him, which later stops a bullet;
    - a stammerer who later delivers a flawless speech (*Shakespeare in Love*);
    - a band of comic-relief allies won over here, who ride in to save the day at the climax.
  - That is why the late rescue is not a deus ex machina: it was earned at 4 (SS104, step 8).
- **It can be compressed or implied.** In Harmon's own five-minute *Call Me Cobra*, the road of trials is just a man sitting down at a table.
  - With 30 more seconds he would have shown the character trying on outfits and personas in a mirror before his meeting.
  - He cut it; the audience infers it (SS106).
- **The symmetrical upward road.** The ascent after step 5 has its own trials, which mirror this descent. The downward road prepares the hero for the "goddess"; the upward one prepares him to rejoin the ordinary world (SS104, step 6).
- **What the StudioBinder Workbook says.**
  - Step 4 is the first attempt to resolve the main tension: the road of trials, leading to the midpoint "Find."
  - It refines the main tension, narrowing it and making it more specific.
  - Its *Dark Knight* example: just as Bruce thinks he can celebrate his retirement, the Joker appears and unleashes a crime wave (SB Workbook, step 4).
  - ⚠️ "Main tension" is sequence-approach vocabulary, not Harmon's.
- **TV version.** The sitcom version reduces this step to "this changes things": the decision of step 3 alters the character's circumstances (SS105).

## Placement
- **On the circle:** the lower-right quarter, from the right-hand crossing (3) down to the bottom point (5). It is in the special world, on the descending, reactive side (SS101; SS104).
- **In time:** the second quarter, together with step 5. Its job is to have the protagonist cope with the altered situation and reach what was lacking. In five minutes, roughly seconds 75 to 150 (SS106). ⚠️ About 25-50%, by our arithmetic.
