**How the beat files are laid out:**
- the source's meaning, in our own words and cited;
- how it works;
- placement;
- equivalents in other frameworks;
- where the composite uses it;
- an empty **"The author's adjusted meaning"** section, to be drafted with the author in the 05:00-14:59 window.

**Copyright check:** every file was checked against the source texts. The highest share of copied 6-word runs is 4.0%, against a target of 8% or less. The repo is public.

## Open items for the author (found 2026-10-29)
1. **Where the master differs from Truby.** These may be deliberate; the author decides:
   - **Fake Ally:** the master's is a good-faith ally; Truby's is a hidden opponent.
   - **Self-Revelation:** the master places it in the second half of Act 2, by default at All Is Lost; Truby places it after the battle, near the end.
   - **Moral Decision:** the master puts it in Dark Night of the Soul with "two (or more)" choices; Truby puts it after the self-revelation, with exactly two.
2. **Arc-template slots:**
   - "BATTLE / KICK IN THE SHINS" sits early in Act 2, while Truby's Battle is the final conflict.
   - "FAKE-ALLY ATTACK" is ambiguous: Truby's step 13 is an attack by a genuine ally acting as the hero's conscience.
3. **Ideas the master lacks entirely.** The biggest:
   - Truby's revelation-and-decision steps (9, 15, 17);
   - the opponent's plan and main counterattack;
   - the hero's moral slide (drive, attack by ally, obsessive drive);
   - the thematic revelation;
   - scene construction and three-track dialogue;
   - endings that keep a story alive;
   - Snyder's ten genres and his set-up/payoff ledger;
   - Harmon's meaningful axes and paired steps.
