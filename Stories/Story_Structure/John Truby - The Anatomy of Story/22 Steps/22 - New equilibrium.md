# New equilibrium (Truby, step 22)

**Source:** John Truby, *The Anatomy of Story*, ch. 8 "Plot", PDF pp. 133-134 (step text and the Casablanca and Tootsie examples); *The Godfather* example p. 142; step list p. 119; thread table p. 120. The new equilibrium is also one of the seven key steps in ch. 3.

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is

When the desire and the need have been met, or tragically left unmet, life returns to normal, with **one big difference**: thanks to what he learned about himself, the hero has moved to a **higher or a lower plane** than where he began (PDF p. 133).

In Truby's table it is a **character** step (PDF p. 120), and it is the last of the **seven key steps**.

## How it works

**Examples:**
- *Casablanca* (PDF p. 134): Rick has recovered his ideals and surrendered his own romance so that others can be free, in service of something larger.
- *Tootsie* (PDF p. 134): Michael has learned honesty, and has grown less self-centered about himself and his work. Telling the truth lets him make peace with Julie and start a genuine romance.
- *The Godfather* (PDF p. 142): Michael has destroyed his enemies and become the new Don, but morally he has fallen and turned into the devil. Once eager to keep clear of the family's violence and crime, he now runs it, ready to murder any traitor or obstacle.

**Two directions.** The equilibrium can be a rise (*Casablanca*, *Tootsie*) or a fall (*The Godfather*). Truby's step text names both, up or down, and both fulfilled and tragically unfulfilled outcomes (PDF p. 133).

**After the twenty-two steps.** Truby closes the step-by-step section here. He says the steps give an almost unlimited ability to build a detailed, organic plot, but take practice, and he ends with two cautions: be flexible about the order, and beware of breaking it too drastically (PDF p. 134). See `08 - Plot.md`, section 7.7.

## Placement

The end of the story, after the moral decision (step 21) (PDF pp. 133-134). It is the final point of the step 1 frame, the place where the hero's change (or failure to change) settles.

## Equivalents in other frameworks

- **Truby, seven key steps:** step 7 of the seven (ch. 3; see `03 - The Seven Key Steps.md`).
- **Snyder:** Final Image, the "after" picture that mirrors the Opening Image.
- **Weiland:** the Resolution (the new normal world).
- **Campbell:** Master of Two Worlds and Freedom to Live. ⚠️ Campbell's stages emphasize the hero's balance between worlds and his freedom rather than a higher or lower level.
- **Harmon:** Change (with Return, the character back home, now changed).
- **Bell:** Transformation, the change shown at the end. ⚠️
