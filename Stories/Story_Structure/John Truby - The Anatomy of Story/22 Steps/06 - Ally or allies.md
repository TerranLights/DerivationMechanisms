# Ally or allies (Truby, step 6)

**Source:** John Truby, *The Anatomy of Story*, ch. 8 "Plot", PDF pp. 124-125 (step text, the Casablanca and Tootsie examples, and the plot technique "Subplot"); *The Godfather* example p. 141; step list p. 119; thread table p. 120. The ally and the subplot character are introduced in ch. 4.

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is

With a desire line in place, the hero usually picks up **one or more allies** to help him beat the opponent and win the goal (PDF p. 124). In Truby's table it is a **character** step (PDF p. 120).

An ally is more than someone for the hero to voice his views to, though that has real use, especially on stage and screen. The ally holds a central place in the character web and is among the writer's chief means of defining the hero (PDF p. 124).

## How it works

**Key point: give the ally a desire of his own.** There is little time to establish an ally, and the fastest way to make the audience feel they are watching a whole person is to give him a goal. Truby's example is the brain-seeking Scarecrow of *The Wizard of Oz* (PDF p. 124).

**Key point: the ally must not outshine the hero.** Truby recalls his premise rule: tell the story of your most interesting character. If the ally is more interesting than the hero, rebuild the story with the ally as hero (PDF p. 124).

**Examples:**
- *Casablanca* (PDF p. 124): Rick's allies are the regulars of his café, each with a set role: the waiter who was once a professor, the Russian behind the bar, the croupier, the doorman-bouncer, and Sam, Rick's piano-playing sidekick.
- *Tootsie* (PDF pp. 124-125): his roommate, Jeff, who is writing a play Michael wants to stage so he can take the lead role.
- *The Godfather* (PDF p. 141): a large set of family allies: his father the Don, Sonny and Fredo, Tom, Clemenza, and Kay.

### Plot technique: subplot (PDF p. 125)

**Definition (from ch. 4).** A subplot has a precise job: to **compare** the hero with a second character facing broadly the same situation.

**Two rules:**
1. **It must affect the main plot, or cut it.** A subplot that doesn't serve the main plot leaves two stories running side by side; the audience may find them clinically interesting, but the main plot will feel overlong. Tie the two together, usually near the end. In *Hamlet*, Laertes (the subplot's lead) joins the main opponent, Claudius, and fights Hamlet in the climactic duel.
2. **Ally and subplot character are normally different people.** Their jobs differ. The ally assists the hero's main line; the subplot character leads a separate, related line that the audience sets against the main one.

**Where true subplots appear.** Most current Hollywood films mix several genres but rarely have a real subplot, because a subplot lengthens the story and those films prize speed. Real subplots show up most in love stories, a form whose main plot tends to be thin. *Moonstruck* has two, one about the heroine's father and one about her mother, and all three lines deal with fidelity in marriage.

**Why it isn't one of the twenty-two.** Usually there is no subplot, and when there is one it is a plot in its own right with its own structure.

**Trade-off.** A subplot enriches character, theme and texture, but it slows the narrative drive of the desire line. The writer has to decide which matters more.

**If you use one.** There is only enough room for it to hit the **seven key steps**, and if it can't hit all seven it will be incomplete and feel forced. Because time is short, bring it in as early as the story naturally allows.

## Placement

After the desire is set (step 5), since allies gather to help pursue it (PDF p. 124). A subplot should start as early as is natural (PDF p. 125).

## Equivalents in other frameworks

- **Campbell:** Supernatural Aid (a helper who equips the hero). ⚠️ Campbell's helper is a mentor or protective figure rather than a companion.
- **Snyder:** the B Story often brings in new characters such as a friend, mentor or love interest. ⚠️ Snyder's B Story is a plot, not an ally function. It also differs from Truby's subplot: Snyder's carries the theme, while Truby's exists for comparison.
- **Bell:** the Care Package (an act of goodwill toward the Lead) sometimes comes from an ally. ⚠️ A loose match.
- **Weiland:** supporting characters; no structural beat. ⚠️
- **Harmon:** no specific step. ⚠️
