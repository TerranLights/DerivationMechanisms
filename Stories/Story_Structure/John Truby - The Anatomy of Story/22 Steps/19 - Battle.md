# Battle (Truby, step 19)

**Source:** John Truby, *The Anatomy of Story*, ch. 8 "Plot", PDF p. 132 (step text and the Casablanca and Tootsie examples); *The Godfather* example p. 142; step list p. 119; thread table p. 120. The battle is also one of the seven key steps in ch. 3.

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is

The **final conflict**, which settles who, if anyone, gets the goal (PDF p. 132). In Truby's table it is a **plot** step (PDF p. 120), and it is the fifth of the **seven key steps**.

## How it works

**Values over firepower.** A huge, violent clash is common but is the least interesting kind of battle: plenty of fireworks, little meaning. The battle should show the audience, as clearly as anything in the story, what each side is fighting for. The question is not which force is stronger but **which ideas or values prevail** (PDF p. 132).

**The funnel point.** Everything converges here. The battle gathers all the characters and all the lines of action, and it happens in the tightest space possible, which raises the sense of conflict and of unbearable pressure (PDF p. 132).

**Need, desire and likeness.** The battle is usually, though not always, where the hero meets his need and gets what he wants. It is also where he most resembles his main opponent, and that very resemblance makes the key differences between them stand out more sharply (PDF p. 132).

**Theme ignites.** In the battle the theme first bursts open for the audience. Watching the values collide, they finally grasp which way of acting and of living is the right one (PDF p. 132).

**Examples:**
- *Casablanca* (PDF p. 132): on the airfield, with Renault at gunpoint, Rick tells Ilsa she will leave with Laszlo. He assures Laszlo of her fidelity, and the couple board the plane. Strasser arrives to stop the takeoff, and Rick shoots him.
- *Tootsie* (PDF p. 132): during a live broadcast, Michael improvises an elaborate storyline revealing that his character is really a man, then pulls off his disguise, stunning both the viewers and the people on set. Afterward Julie punches him and walks out. Truby notes that the fight between Michael and Julie amounts to little (one punch); a big reveal, the unmasking before cast, crew and viewers across the country, takes the place of a big fight. He also admires that the plot Michael invents for his character follows the same path of female liberation he has gone through by living as a woman.
- *The Godfather* (PDF p. 142): the battle crosscuts Michael standing as godfather at his nephew's baptism with the killings of the five families' bosses. In church he professes belief in God and, following the rite, renounces Satan, while his men gun down his rivals one after another (Moe Green and Barzini among them); Tessio is taken away to be killed, and Carlo is strangled on Michael's orders.

**Variations.** A battle of values with little violence (*Casablanca*); a reveal replacing a fight (*Tootsie*); a crosscut that sets ritual against slaughter (*The Godfather*).

## Placement

Near the end: after step 18 (gate, gauntlet, visit to death), which often triggers it, and before the self-revelation (step 20), which comes out of its crucible (PDF pp. 132-133). The gauntlet can also run during the battle (PDF p. 132). In the *Godfather* example the moral decision (step 21) comes just before the battle rather than after it (PDF p. 142).
