# Inciting event (Truby, step 4)

**Source:** John Truby, *The Anatomy of Story*, ch. 8 "Plot", PDF pp. 123-124 (step text and the Casablanca and Tootsie examples); *The Godfather* example p. 141; step list p. 119; thread table p. 120.

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is

An event that comes **from outside** the hero and makes him form a goal and start acting (PDF p. 123). In Truby's table it is a **plot** step (PDF p. 120).

Truby calls it a small step with one important exception: it **links need and desire**. While the opening sets up weakness and need, the hero is usually stuck or paralyzed in some way, and something has to jolt him out of that and force him to act (PDF pp. 123-124).

## How it works

**Key point: out of the frying pan, into the fire.** Truby offers this old saying as the guide to finding the best inciting event (PDF p. 124). The strongest version makes the hero believe he has finally got clear of the crisis that dogged him from the opening, when in fact it has landed him in deeper trouble than he has ever known.

**Examples:**
- *Sunset Boulevard:* Joe, an out-of-work screenwriter, flees two men sent to repossess his car. A tire blows, he swerves into a driveway (Norma Desmond's) and believes he's safe; actually he has walked into a trap he will never get out of (PDF p. 124).
- *Casablanca:* Ilsa and Laszlo walk into Rick's café. These two newcomers will jolt Rick out of his stable, masterful and unhappy position (PDF p. 124).
- *Tootsie:* George, Michael's agent, says nobody will hire him because he is so hard to work with. That pushes Michael to dress as a woman and audition for a soap opera (PDF p. 124).
- *The Godfather:* a newspaper report that his father has been gunned down ends Michael's separateness from the family (PDF p. 141).

## Placement

After the opening (steps 2-3), which establishes the paralysis it breaks, and before the desire (step 5), which it brings into being (PDF pp. 123-124). Truby gives no page or percentage.

## Equivalents in other frameworks

- **Snyder:** Catalyst.
- **Bell:** Disturbance, as the composite treats it. ⚠️ Bell places a disturbance on the opening pages, so it may sit earlier than Truby's inciting event.
- **Campbell:** Call to Adventure.
- **Weiland:** Inciting Event (same name; roughly the middle of her first act).
- **Harmon:** between You and Need, or the trigger inside Need. ⚠️
- **Truby's own example** (*Sunset Boulevard*) also fits Snyder's and Campbell's sense of an event that seems like rescue but traps the hero. ⚠️ An interpretation, not stated by any of them.
