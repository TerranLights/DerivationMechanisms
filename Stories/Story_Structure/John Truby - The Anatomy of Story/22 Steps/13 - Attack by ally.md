# Attack by ally (Truby, step 13)

**Source:** John Truby, *The Anatomy of Story*, ch. 8 "Plot", PDF p. 129 (step text and the Casablanca and Tootsie examples); *The Godfather* example pp. 141-142; step list p. 119; thread table p. 120. For writing the moral dialogue of this scene Truby refers to ch. 10.

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is

As the drive goes on, the hero falls behind and grows desperate. When he begins taking **immoral steps** to succeed, his **ally confronts him** (PDF p. 129). In Truby's table it is a **moral argument** step (PDF p. 120).

**The ally as conscience.** At this point the ally becomes the hero's conscience. In effect he says: I'm on your side and want you to win, but your methods are wrong (PDF p. 129).

**The hero's response.** Usually the hero defends what he is doing and rejects the criticism (PDF p. 129).

**Its function.** It adds a **second level of conflict** to the story (the first being hero against opposition). It adds to the strain on the hero and pushes him to start questioning his values and how he acts (PDF p. 129).

## How it works

**Two parts in the examples:** the ally's **criticism** and the hero's **justification**.

**Examples:**
- *Casablanca* (PDF p. 129): the criticism comes not from one of Rick's allies but from his first opponent, Ilsa. At the market she says he has changed from the man she loved in Paris; when he makes a crude pass at her, she reveals she was already Laszlo's wife before their affair. *Justification:* Rick has none, beyond pleading that he was drunk the previous night.
- *Tootsie* (PDF p. 129): when Michael fakes illness to dodge Sandy and spend the weekend at Julie's family farm, his roommate Jeff asks how long he plans to go on lying to people. *Justification:* Michael claims that lying to a woman beats hurting her with the truth.
- *The Godfather* (PDF pp. 141-142): back from Sicily, Michael hears Kay object to his joining his father's business; she insists he isn't that kind of man. *Justification:* he promises that within five years the family's business will be entirely lawful.

**Variation.** The *Casablanca* example shows the critic need not be an ally in the strict sense; there, the lover-opponent plays the conscience role.

## Placement

During the drive (step 12), once the hero has started acting immorally, and before the apparent defeat (step 14) (PDF p. 129).
