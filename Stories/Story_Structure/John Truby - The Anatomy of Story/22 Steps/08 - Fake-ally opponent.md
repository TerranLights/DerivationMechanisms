# Fake-ally opponent (Truby, step 8)

**Source:** John Truby, *The Anatomy of Story*, ch. 8 "Plot", PDF p. 126 (step text and the Casablanca and Tootsie examples); *The Godfather* example p. 141; step list p. 119; thread table p. 120.

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is

Someone who **seems to be on the hero's side** but is **really an opponent**, or is working for the main opponent (PDF p. 126). In Truby's table it is a **character** step (PDF p. 120).

## How it works

**Why it strengthens the plot.** Reveals drive plot, and reveals come from the hero's efforts to uncover the real strength of the opposition. Each new discovery about an opponent turns the plot, to the audience's delight. The fake-ally opponent adds to the opposition's power precisely because his opposition is hidden; he makes hero and audience dig below the visible tip of the iceberg (step 7) to learn what the hero is really facing (PDF p. 126).

**Why it is a rich character.** This figure is complex by nature and often changes in an interesting way during the story. By acting as the hero's friend he begins to feel like one, and so he ends up caught in a dilemma: he serves the opponent while hoping the hero wins (PDF p. 126).

**When to introduce him.** Normally he follows the main opponent, though there are exceptions: if the opponent's plan against the hero was laid before the story opens, the fake ally can come first (PDF p. 126).

**Examples:**
- *Casablanca* (PDF p. 126): Captain Renault is always charming and friendly toward Rick, but he looks after himself by serving the Nazis. He is far more open about his opposition than most fake allies, who work in secret. At the very end he switches sides and becomes Rick's true ally, one of the film's great pleasures, and Truby uses it to show how much storytelling power a switch between ally and opponent (in either direction) can release.
- *Tootsie* (PDF p. 126): Sandy is also an unusual case, since she doesn't deceive hero and audience from the outset. She starts as Michael's actress friend and turns into a fake-ally opponent once he, disguised as a woman, auditions for the soap role she was after herself. When she walks in on him wearing her clothes, he has to deepen the lie by pretending he has fallen for her.
- *The Godfather* (PDF p. 141): Michael faces a remarkably high count of fake allies, which enlarges the plot considerably: the driver on the day his father is shot; his Sicilian bodyguard, whose attempt on Michael kills Michael's wife; Carlo, the sister's husband, who sets Sonny up to be murdered; and Tessio, who defects to Barzini.

## Placement

Usually after the main opponent (step 7), sometimes before (PDF p. 126). The audience often learns the fake ally's true identity at the audience revelation (step 16), and the hero often learns it at the third revelation (step 17) (PDF p. 131).

The master describes an established ally who later acts against the protagonist out of his own genuine interests, and stresses that this is not betrayal.
