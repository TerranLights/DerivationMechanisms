# Third revelation and decision (Truby, step 17)

**Source:** John Truby, *The Anatomy of Story*, ch. 8 "Plot", PDF p. 131 (step text and the Casablanca and Tootsie examples); *The Godfather* example p. 142; step list p. 119; thread table p. 120.

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is

One more stage in the hero's education about what beating the opponent will take (PDF p. 131). In Truby's table it is a **plot** step (PDF p. 120).

**Catching up with the audience.** Where there is a fake-ally opponent, the hero frequently uncovers his true identity at this step, the thing the audience already learned at step 16 (PDF p. 131).

**Stronger, not weaker.** You might expect a hero who keeps discovering how powerful the opposition really is to want out of the fight. Truby says the reverse happens: the knowledge makes him feel stronger and more determined, because at last he can see the full scale of what he faces (PDF p. 131).

## How it works

Truby's examples use the same set of elements as step 9: revelation, decision, changed desire, changed motive. Desire and motive can stay unchanged.

**Examples:**
- *Casablanca* (PDF p. 131):
  - *Revelation:* Ilsa comes to Rick to get the letters and admits she has never stopped loving him.
  - *Decision:* he resolves to give the letters of transit to Ilsa and Laszlo, but keeps the decision secret from Ilsa and from the audience.
  - *Changed desire:* to get the couple safely beyond Nazi reach.
  - *Changed motive:* he knows Ilsa has to leave with Laszlo and support his cause.
- *Tootsie* (PDF p. 131):
  - *Revelation:* Michael passes on to Sandy a box of chocolates that was Les's gift to Dorothy, and Sandy denounces him as a fake who lies.
  - *Decision:* he will see his agent and somehow get out of the contract.
  - *Desire and motive:* both unchanged. He still wants off the show, and he still can't keep lying to everyone.
  - *Added revelation:* after Dorothy brings Julie a present, Julie says they must stop seeing each other, since continuing would mislead Dorothy. *Decision:* Michael will confess the masquerade. *Desire:* unchanged (Julie). *Motive:* he loves Julie and understands he can never have her while playing Dorothy.
- *The Godfather* (PDF p. 142):
  - *Revelation:* Michael realizes Tessio has defected and that Barzini is planning his murder.
  - *Decision:* to strike first.
  - *Changed desire:* to destroy all his enemies in a single blow.
  - *Changed motive:* to end the war for good.

**Variation: hidden decisions.** In *Casablanca*, the decision is concealed even from the audience. That sets up the final reveal in the battle (see step 12's note on Rick's two goals and step 19).

## Placement

After the audience revelation (step 16) and before step 18 (gate, gauntlet, visit to death) and the battle (step 19), in Truby's typical order (PDF pp. 119, 131). Revelations should come faster toward the end, so this one tends to arrive close behind the previous steps (see the revelations sequence in `08 - Plot.md`).

## Equivalents in other frameworks

- **Snyder:** Break into Three (the A and B stories meet and supply the solution), or, in the Finale, the High Tower Surprise. ⚠️
- **Bell:** Doorway of No Return #2, which can be a discovery that makes the final battle inevitable. ⚠️
- **Weiland:** the move from the Third Plot Point into the Climax. ⚠️
- **Campbell, Harmon:** no clear match. ⚠️
