# Second revelation and decision (obsessive drive; changed desire, changed motive) (Truby, step 15)

**Source:** John Truby, *The Anatomy of Story*, ch. 8 "Plot", PDF pp. 130-131 (step text and the Casablanca and Tootsie examples); *The Godfather* example p. 142; step list p. 119; thread table p. 120.

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is

Directly following the apparent defeat, the hero nearly always gets **another major revelation**. Without it the defeat would be real and the story would end. The new information shows him he **can still win**, and he decides to re-enter the fight and take up his quest again (PDF p. 130).

In Truby's table the revelation and decision belong to the **plot** thread, and the obsessive drive and changed desire and motive to the **character** thread (PDF p. 120).

**From desire to obsession.** The revelation galvanizes the hero. Before, he wanted the goal (desire and drive); from here on it obsesses him and will do nearly anything to win. Truby says the hero turns **tyrannical** in pursuit of victory (PDF p. 130).

**The moral slide continues.** The new information strengthens him, but he also keeps sliding in the moral decline that started during the drive, which advances the moral argument a further step (PDF p. 130).

**Direction changes again.** The revelation also changes his desire and motive, and the story swings in a new direction (PDF p. 130).

## How it works

**All five, or it fails.** Make sure all five elements occur (the revelation, the decision, the obsession, and the shifts in desire and motive). If one is missing, the moment goes flat and the plot sags (PDF p. 130).

**Examples:**
- *Casablanca* (PDF p. 130):
  - *Revelation:* Ilsa tells Rick she was already Laszlo's wife before they met, which is why she left him in Paris.
  - *Decision:* he makes no obvious one, though he informs Renault that he alone will be the one to use the letters.
  - *Changed desire:* he no longer wants to hurt her.
  - *Obsessive drive:* an unusual case. Rick's first burst of obsession came earlier, when Ilsa reappeared and he was desperate to hurt her as she had hurt him. He starts at a far higher pitch of passion than most heroes, but even that level has room to rise, since by the end he is leaving to join the fight to save the world. Truby adds that Rick only *seems* to sink further morally; in fact he has already decided to get Ilsa and Laszlo out together and is set on doing it.
  - *Changed motive:* he has forgiven Ilsa.
- *Tootsie* (PDF pp. 130-131):
  - *Revelation:* the producer tells Dorothy they want her for another year.
  - *Decision:* Michael will get George to break the contract.
  - *Changed desire:* to be rid of the burden of his disguise and grow closer to Julie.
  - *Obsessive drive:* he is set on getting free of Dorothy.
  - *Changed motive:* mounting guilt over how kindly Julie and Les treat him.
  - *Added revelation:* Les proposes to Dorothy; Dorothy walks out on him at the bar; Michael now wants to stop deceiving Les; his motive is unchanged (guilt).
  - Truby notes that Michael's moral decline deepens here despite his guilt and his efforts to escape: the longer the charade lasts, the more he hurts the people around him.
- *The Godfather* (PDF p. 142):
  - *Revelation:* Michael realizes there is a bomb in the car and his wife is about to turn the key.
  - *Decision:* he tries to stop her, too late.
  - *Changed desire:* to return to his family in America.
  - *Obsessive drive:* he is bent on avenging his wife and his brother.
  - *Changed motive:* their killers have to pay.

**Variation.** Added revelations can cluster here (the *Tootsie* proposal). The obsessive drive may appear earlier than this step in a hero who starts at high passion (*Casablanca*).

## Placement

Immediately after the apparent defeat (step 14), so around or just past the two-thirds to three-quarters mark (PDF pp. 129-130). It comes before the audience revelation (step 16).

## Equivalents in other frameworks

- **Snyder:** the end of Dark Night of the Soul leading to Break into Three, where a new idea shows the way forward. ⚠️ Snyder's turn is toward wisdom, not obsession, and he has no moral-decline element.
- **Bell:** Q Factor (something renews the hero's capacity to act) and/or Doorway of No Return #2. ⚠️
- **Campbell:** Rescue from Without or Crossing the Return Threshold. ⚠️ Weak match.
- **Weiland:** the turn from the Third Plot Point into the third act. ⚠️
- **Harmon:** between Take and Return. ⚠️
