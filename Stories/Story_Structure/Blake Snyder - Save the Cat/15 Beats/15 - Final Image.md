# Final Image (Save the Cat, beat 15)

Source: Blake Snyder, *Save the Cat!* (2005), ch. 4 "Let's Beat It Out!" (the Final Image and Opening Image sections, and the *Miss Congeniality* breakdown); also ch. 5 "Building the Perfect Beast" (+/- scenes; row 4 of The Board), ch. 7 "What's Wrong with This Picture?" ## What it is
The Final Image is the last thing the film shows, and it should **reverse the Opening Image**. It serves as the writer's evidence that change has happened and that the change is real (Snyder, ch. 4).

Snyder presents the two images as bookends. The opening is a "before" snapshot, this is the "after," and the gap between them, a plus set against a minus, records the emotional upheaval the film is about (Snyder, ch. 4).

## How it works
- **Use it as a diagnostic.** If you have no final image, or can't see how one would apply, recheck your math: something in Act Two isn't adding up (Snyder, ch. 4). A missing or weak Final Image signals that the middle of the story hasn't produced real change.
- **The change must be visible at a glance.** Snyder warns that actors often read just the first and last ten pages of a script to see whether a dramatic change is there. If it isn't, the script may be thrown aside (Snyder, ch. 4). The Final Image is half of that test.
- **The farther back you start, the stronger the ending.** The "Take a Step Back" rule asks the writer to start the hero, and every character, as far back emotionally as possible, so the audience sees the whole journey. Snyder compares it to drawing a bow all the way back so the arrow flies farthest (Snyder, ch. 7). The distance between the Opening and Final Images depends on that starting point.
- **It is the model for the scene rule.** In Board work, every scene must swing emotionally from plus to minus or the reverse, mirroring the way a film's first and last images oppose each other (Snyder, ch. 5). The Final Image completes the film-wide version of that swing.
- **Last payoffs can land here ⚠️.** The Glossary says a running gag should get a twist late in the film once the character has changed, as in the coffee drinker who finally orders tea (Snyder, Glossary). Snyder doesn't tie this to the Final Image specifically; placing it here is our inference.

**Example breakdown (Snyder, ch. 4, *Miss Congeniality*):** the film ends on the reverse of its first image. The tomboy who opened the film surrounded by boys now stands among women, who vote her their Miss Congeniality. Snyder adds, as a joke, that the "real happy ending" was the film's $100 million domestic box office.
