# Break into Three (Save the Cat, beat 13)

Source: Blake Snyder, *Save the Cat!* (2005), ch. 4 "Let's Beat It Out!" (the Break into Three section and the *Miss Congeniality* breakdown); also ch. 5 "Building the Perfect Beast" ("The Major Turns," "The Eternally Light Act Three") and the Glossary ("Thesis, Antithesis, Synthesis," "Major Turns," "Booster Rocket"). Cited below as (Snyder, ch. N).

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is
The Break into Three is the moment the solution appears (Snyder, ch. 4). Snyder credits it to three things:
- the people of the B story (the love story);
- the conversations about theme that happen in the B story;
- the hero's final all-out attempt to defeat the opposition that has been gaining on him, and beating him, in the A story.

Here the external story (A) and the internal story (B) meet and twine together. The hero has come through every test and dug deep for the answer; all that's left is to apply it. A way to solve the problem now exists, and the third world, **synthesis**, is about to begin (Snyder, ch. 4).

## How it works
- **A and B fuse.** Snyder's classic version has the hero get the key clue from "the girl," and that clue solves both problems at once: defeating the villains and winning the beloved (Snyder, ch. 4). The B story's love and theme strands supply what the A story's action needs.
- **It opens the synthesis.** In Snyder's three-world model, Act One is the thesis and Act Two the upside-down antithesis. By mastering that topsy-turvy world, the hero gains what he needs to combine the old world with its opposite into something new. That synthesis happens in Act Three, which this beat opens (Snyder, ch. 4; Glossary, "Thesis, Antithesis, Synthesis").
- **It follows the Dark Night.** The hero, beaten and aware of it, finally works the answer out by page 85 (Snyder, ch. 4).
- **It is a major turn.** On The Board it is the hinge at the end of row 3, the last of the three places where the story swings in a new direction (Snyder, ch. 5; Glossary, "Major Turns").
- **It comes easily once the others are set.** Snyder places the Midpoint first, then All Is Lost as its flip. With those two set, he finds this third hinge usually comes easily (Snyder, ch. 5).
- **Early boards show it as one card.** In first passes, Act Three is usually just two cards: one where the hero works out what to do and one for the showdown. Snyder says it always fills out in time (Snyder, ch. 5). The first of those two cards is, in effect, this beat.
- **Watch for a sag just before it.** The Glossary's "Booster Rocket" entry names the end of Act Two as a likely slow patch. *Home Alone* livens up the mother's journey home there by bringing in a polka-band musician (Snyder, Glossary).

**Example breakdown (Snyder, ch. 4, *Miss Congeniality*):** the friends she has made among the contestants fix her up for the final night of the pageant. Accepted by the world that once felt alien to her, and sure the women truly care about her, the heroine comes back to life. In helping her, the contestants help themselves too.

## Placement
- Page 85 of a 110-page script (Snyder, ch. 4). Our conversion: about 77%.
- On The Board it sits at the end of row 3 (pages 55-85), the third hinge. Row 4 (pages 85-110) is Act Three (Snyder, ch. 5).
