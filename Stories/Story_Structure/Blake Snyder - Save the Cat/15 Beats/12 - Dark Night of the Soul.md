# Dark Night of the Soul (Save the Cat, beat 12)

Source: Blake Snyder, *Save the Cat!* (2005), ch. 4 "Let's Beat It Out!" (the Dark Night of the Soul section and the *Miss Congeniality* breakdown); also ch. 2 "Give Me the Same Thing... Only Different!" (Rites of Passage) and ch. 5 "Building the Perfect Beast" (row 3 of The Board). Cited below as (Snyder, ch. N).

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is
All Is Lost delivers the blow, often a death. This beat answers the next question: how does the hero *feel* about it? (Snyder, ch. 4).

It is the darkness just before dawn: the moment before the hero reaches down inside and finds the final, decisive idea that rescues him and the people around him. For now, though, no such idea is in sight (Snyder, ch. 4).

## How it works
- **Any length, but always present.** The beat may be a few seconds long or run several minutes. Snyder insists it must be there and that it is essential (Snyder, ch. 4).
- **The forsaken moment.** Snyder admits he doesn't know exactly why audiences need to see it, only that they do. He names it after Christ's cry of abandonment on the cross ("why hast thou forsaken me"). This continues the crucifixion image he used for the All Is Lost death (Snyder, ch. 4).
- **It works because it is primal.** Everyone has been there (Snyder, ch. 4):
  - **His image of it:** stuck by the road with a flat, almost no money, late for a meeting that could change everything, and feeling hopeless and foolish.
  - **What turns it:** the answer comes only once we own our limits and our human weakness and let fate, not ourselves, steer what happens.
  - **The rule:** the hero must be beaten, and know he is beaten, before he can learn the lesson.
- **It belongs to every genre.** It appears in comedies as well as dramas, because it is true to life and everyone recognizes it (Snyder, ch. 4).
- **It ends in the realization.** By page 85 the hero finally works it out, which carries the story into the Break into Three (Snyder, ch. 4).
- **A genre echo ⚠️.** Snyder's Rites of Passage genre ends in a similar surrender. The victory there is won by giving in to forces stronger than ourselves and accepting our humanity (Snyder, ch. 2). He doesn't link this to the Dark Night himself; the parallel is ours.

**Example breakdown (Snyder, ch. 4, *Miss Congeniality*):** the heroine turns up for the pageant finale a complete mess. She is caught between two identities, no longer an FBI agent and not yet a woman at ease in this world, and doesn't know what to do.
