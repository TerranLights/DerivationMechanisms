# Break into Two (Save the Cat, beat 6)

Source: Blake Snyder, *Save the Cat!* (2005), ch. 4 "Let's Beat It Out!" (the Break into Two section, the Set-Up and B Story sections, and the *Miss Congeniality* breakdown); also ch. 5 "Building the Perfect Beast" (The Board's hinges, "The Major Turns," "Final Words on the Subject"), ch. 6 "The Immutable Laws of Screenplay Physics" (Laying Pipe), ch. 7 "What's Wrong with This Picture?" (The Hero Leads), and the Glossary ("Major Turns," "Thesis, Antithesis, Synthesis"). Cited below as (Snyder, ch. N).

Research extraction, 2026-10-29. The source's ideas in our own words; not the author's adjusted version.

## What it is
The Break into Two is the first act break. The hero leaves the old world behind and enters a new one (Snyder, ch. 4).

In Snyder's three-world model, Act One is the **thesis**, the world as it is. The Break into Two crosses into the **antithesis**, an upside-down version of that world. Because the two worlds are so different, the crossing has to be clear-cut, not gradual (Snyder, ch. 4; Glossary, "Thesis, Antithesis, Synthesis").

## How it works
- **Something big must happen.** When Snyder picks up someone else's script, page 25 is the first place he turns. He checks whether anything happens there and whether the writer knows that something should, and he means something major (Snyder, ch. 4).
- **Don't negotiate the page.** He has argued with writers who want it on page 28 or 30, and his answer is no: in a 110-page script it comes by page 25 (Snyder, ch. 4).
- **The hero must choose.** This is the central rule. In Snyder's early drafts the act break is often vague, with circumstances carrying the hero into Act Two. He calls that wrong. The hero can't be enticed, tricked or allowed to drift across; he has to make the decision himself, and that initiative is part of what makes him a hero (Snyder, ch. 4). The same principle runs through "The Hero Leads," which insists the hero be proactive throughout (Snyder, ch. 7).
- **Example: *Star Wars*.** A killing at home is the event that sets Luke going, but the choice to leave is his. He can't simply wake up on the smuggler's ship with no idea how he got there (Snyder, ch. 4). Snyder says Luke's "parents" are killed; in the film it is his aunt and uncle ⚠️.
- **The crossing is abrupt, so cushion it.** Snyder describes the jump into Act Two as obvious, even jarring. The B story, starting around page 30, acts as a small "booster rocket" that eases the transition by shifting attention to something new (Snyder, ch. 4; Glossary, "Booster Rocket").
- **Don't take too long to get here.** The Laying Pipe law says a setup that needs more than 25 pages is a problem (Snyder, ch. 6).
- **It is one of the major turns.** On The Board, each of the first three rows ends on a hinge where the story turns: this beat, then the Midpoint, then the act break into Three. Snyder links this to Syd Field's view that every turn sends the plot off on a fresh heading (Snyder, ch. 5).
  - **Building order.** After placing your favorite scenes, you place the major turns. Snyder finds most writers can pin down the Break into Two easily, because the setup and the start of the adventure are what they already picture. The harder question is where the story goes next, which the Midpoint answers (Snyder, ch. 5).
  - **What to remember.** In his closing advice on The Board, hitting the act break on page 25 is one of the few things he wants a writer to remember while drafting (Snyder, ch. 5).

**Example breakdown (Snyder, ch. 4, *Miss Congeniality*):** the heroine emerges from her makeover looking glamorous and impresses her colleague, then trips. It won't be easy, but she is ready, and Act Two begins.

## Placement
- Page 25 of a 110-page script, and no later (Snyder, ch. 4). Our conversion: about 23%.
- On The Board it sits at the end of row 1 (Act One, pages 1-25), the first hinge (Snyder, ch. 5).
- **Labeling slip in the source:** the Glossary's "Major Turns" entry lists "the break into Act One" alongside the midpoint and Act Three break. This appears to mean the break into Act Two ⚠️.

## Equivalents in other frameworks
- **Campbell:** Crossing the First Threshold. Belly of the Whale may follow just after ⚠️.
- **Bell:** Doorway of No Return #1.
- **Weiland:** the First Plot Point, at about 25%.
- **Harmon:** step 3, Go.
- **Truby:** step 9, First revelation and decision ⚠️. In Truby, decisions follow revelations throughout, so this is only a loose match.
- **Snyder's own set:** the other major turns, the Midpoint and the Break into Three.
