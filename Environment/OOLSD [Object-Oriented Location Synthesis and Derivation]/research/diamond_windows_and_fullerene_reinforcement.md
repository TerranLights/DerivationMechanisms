# Diamond windows and fullerene-family reinforcement for orbital-habitat glazing

Tranche 9, section A3. Research agent output, started 2026-11-01. Physics and engineering only.

## Scope

Lab-grown diamond (CVD and HPHT) as a pressure-window material for a crewed orbital habitat, compared with sapphire, ALON, spinel, fused silica, borosilicate, acrylic and polycarbonate; how diamond is mounted and joined; which fullerene-family materials (C60, C70, higher fullerenes including C64, fullerene polymers, carbon nanotubes, graphene fibers) can form load-bearing fibers or cables, at what measured macroscopic strength; and where cable or frame reinforcement helps a pressure window. The developer's *Graphene Science Handbook* (CRC Press, 2016, six volumes) is used as a primary source alongside the web.

**Where cable reinforcement helps a pressure window, and where it does not** (reading of F9, F10 with D3, D9, D10):
- *Helps:* carrying the hull's global loads (hoop and axial tension, spin load) around and across a window region as tension in a fiber or cable grid that is allowed to curve between supports (D9). Each transparent pane then spans only one grid cell, and its thickness falls in proportion to the cell size (D3). This is the mullion-and-frame logic of the Cupola and of small-pane mosaics, and the restraint-layer logic of inflatable modules (not confirmed for windows; Open threads). **ENG.**
- *Helps:* fail-safety. A second pane, an interlayer that holds fragments (ionoplast, F10) or a restraint grid can keep pressure after one pane cracks; NASA-STD-5018 requires a redundant pane able to hold full pressure on its own. **DEM.**
- *Does not help:* a flat net cannot hold pressure without sag (N = P L^2/(8 f)); a taut flat net over a window either bulges by a tenth of its span or carries very large tension (D9). **ENG (statics).**
- *Does not help:* fibers inside the pane. Fullerene-family fibers are opaque (D10); index matching to diamond is impossible with known fibers and matrices (F10); and in a brittle pane the governing failure is a surface flaw under bending (F2, D2), which internal fibers do not prevent. **ENG.**
- *Does not help:* stiffening a diamond pane with a rigid metal or high-expansion frame: thermal mismatch alone can break it (D6). **ENG.**

## Conflicts

1. **Polycrystalline diamond strength.** Balmer et al. (2009): growth side 557 MPa (optical, m 23.1) and nucleation side 1,151 MPa at 500 um. Element Six brochure (2019): growth 400 MPa (optical) and 300 MPa (RF), nucleation 800 MPa. Diamond Materials: 750 MPa (500 um, nucleation). Likely causes: thickness (strength falls with thickness as surface grains coarsen), grade, and test geometry; the brochure does not state the thickness.
2. **Single-crystal diamond strength.** Balmer: CVD mean 2,860 MPa (m 2.55). E6 brochure: 3,500 MPa (m 2.5). Both show the same very low Weibull modulus; the difference is within the scatter (standard deviation 1,200 MPa).
3. **Diamond erosion-yield uncertainty.** NASA/TM-2018-219756 gives +/- 0.04 in the abstract and +/- 0.40 x 10^-26 in the conclusions. One of these is a typographical error; the source does not say which.
4. **Fused silica strength.** Corning: 52.4 MPa abraded modulus of rupture. Surmet's two tables: 110 MPa (16 ksi) and 48 MPa (7 ksi). Glass strength depends on the surface flaw population (abraded vs polished), stressed area and loading rate; it is not a single material constant (NASA-STD-5018 uses flaw-growth analysis for this reason).
5. **Sapphire.** Kyocera 690 MPa and 41 W/m K; Surmet 742 MPa (107.7 ksi) and 36 W/m K; Surmet's other table gives 100 ksi (689 MPa). Orientation, surface finish and test method differ.
6. **Spinel strength.** Surmet's table 184 MPa (26.7 ksi) vs a patent's >= 300 MPa for fine-grained spinel: grain size (S22 summary).
7. **Window formula constant.** Harris/Yoder K = 1.25 (unclamped), 0.75 (clamped); Tydex 1.1; plate theory 3(3+nu)/8 = 1.16 (nu 0.1) to 1.24 (nu 0.3) for a simply supported center. Differences are the assumed edge condition and Poisson's ratio.
8. **Polycrystalline graphene strength (handbook).** Vol 4 Ch 4 cites simulations: grain boundaries halve strength (about 46 GPa). Vol 4 Ch 7 cites measurements: 85% retained for 1-5 um grains. Simulated boundaries versus real, well-stitched boundaries.
9. **Graphene fiber record.** 3.4 GPa (Li et al. 2020) vs 842.6 MPa called "the highest ever recorded" in 2016 (Zhang et al.): epoch; the field moved four-fold in four years.
10. **Fullerene nanowhisker modulus.** 8.9 GPa (in solution) vs 28 +/- 5 GPa (bct structure): structure and measurement medium.
11. **Pioneer Venus diamond window size.** My search string assumed 13.8 mm; the only dimensional source found gives 15.4 mm x 2.5 mm (secondary). The 15.4 mm figure is used.
12. **Olson et al. (1992) modulus.** The abstract prints "18 MPa" vs "2 MPa" for PMMA; PMMA's modulus is 3.1 GPa per the Arkema sheet, so GPa is meant.

**D2. What the Weibull modulus does to design strength.** Strength at failure probability Pf relative to the characteristic strength: (-ln(1-Pf))^(1/m). Area scaling per tenfold increase in stressed area: 10^(-1/m). Inputs: Weibull moduli from Balmer et al. (2009) and E6.
```

poly growth E6 m=15:   Pf=0.001: 0.631; Pf=1e-06: 0.398; per decade of area 0.858
poly growth m=23.1:    Pf=0.001: 0.742; Pf=1e-06: 0.550; per decade of area 0.905
```
Converting means to characteristic strengths (sigma0 = mean / Gamma(1 + 1/m)) and then to a 1-in-1,000 failure probability:
```
SC CVD diamond: mean 2860 MPa, m 2.55: characteristic 3222 MPa; strength at Pf=1e-3 215 MPa
poly growth E6: mean 400 MPa, m 15: characteristic 414 MPa; strength at Pf=1e-3 261 MPa
poly growth optical Balmer: mean 557 MPa, m 23.1: characteristic 570 MPa; strength at Pf=1e-3 423 MPa
```
So at the same reliability single-crystal diamond (215 MPa) is *weaker* than polycrystalline growth-side material (261-423 MPa), despite a mean seven times higher. Each tenfold increase in pane area cuts single-crystal strength by 60%, polycrystalline growth-side strength by 10-14%. Basis: two-parameter Weibull, weakest-link volume/area scaling; the test specimens' effective areas are not given in the sources, so the absolute area correction for a full-size pane is not computed.

## Constraints for any inhabitant

- A single diamond pane is at most about 9 cm across (single crystal) or 13.5-18 cm (polycrystalline) today (F1). A large diamond window is therefore a framed mosaic of small tiles; every tile needs a frame, seal and joint, and frames block part of the view (D7).
- An uncoated diamond surface reflects about 17% of light per side, about a third per pane (D1); with the redundant second pane that NASA-STD-5018 requires (F10), uncoated diamond would pass about half the light. Anti-reflection coatings become part of the life-support hardware.
- Diamond transmits ultraviolet down to about 220 nm (F2); it is not a UV filter, so another layer must block whatever solar ultraviolet the occupants and plants should not receive.
- Diamond's design strength depends on its flaw statistics, not its hardness: single crystals have such scattered strength (Weibull modulus about 2.5) that a reliable pane is weaker than a polycrystalline one (D2).
- At equal safety factor, a diamond pane is not lighter per square meter than sapphire or ALON, and is heavier than acrylic or polycarbonate (D3). Diamond's advantages are hardness, heat conduction and chemical resistance, not mass.
- A diamond pane in a rigid metal frame breaks from temperature swings of a few tens of kelvin (D6); compliant mounts or low-expansion frames are mandatory.
- Ram-facing diamond erodes slowly (0.45 um/yr at ISS-like exposure) but roughens into sub-micron cones (D5): expect gradual haze, so outer panes should be sacrificial and replaceable, as NASA's debris-pane rule already requires (F10).
- Fullerene-family fibers are opaque (D10): any cable crossing a window is a dark line in the view; cables belong at mullions and frame lines, not inside the glazing.
- One atmosphere presses 101 kN (the weight of 10.3 metric tons) on every square meter of window (101,325 Pa / 9.80665 m/s2); pane mass grows linearly with span (D3), and in a large hull the hoop load cannot pass through the glazing (D9). That load must be led around the window into the hull through a frame or fiber grid, as aircraft and spacecraft windows already do.

## Candidate handwaves

Flagged, not accepted; the developer decides.

1. **Monolithic diamond panes larger than about 20 cm.** No such plate exists (F1). Large single-crystal growth by heteroepitaxy is at 3 inches in production and 92 mm as a record; polycrystalline is at 135-180 mm. A meter-class diamond pane requires extrapolating plate diameter 5.6-7.4-fold and optical-grade thickness four- to five-fold (D7), with no published path.
2. **"C64/Fullerene cables" as a material.** C64 exists only as the hydrogenated molecule C64H4 in milligram quantities (F8); bare C64 is computational. No fiber, cable or polymer of C64 is reported anywhere found, and the developer's six-volume handbook never mentions C64. Treating C64 as a structural fiber is a handwave. The physically grounded reading of "fullerene cables" is **carbon-nanotube fiber** (CNTs are the tubular members of the same family, F8), whose best macroscopic strength is 8.2 GPa (F9).
3. **Nanoscale strength at cable length.** Using single-tube or single-sheet values (100-130 GPa) for meter-scale cables bends physics as currently demonstrated: real fibers reach 1-8% of them, and strength falls further with gauge length (F9, D8). Only cm-long bundles of perfect tubes have shown 80 GPa.
4. **Fibers embedded in a diamond window.** No diamond-matrix fiber composite exists; an index-matched transparent composite needs refractive indices within 0.002 (F10), and nothing fibrous matches diamond's 2.39; CNT and graphene fibers are opaque regardless (D10).
5. **Large diamond windows at low cost.** No public cost per area exists for optical-grade plate (F5); gem prices per carat do not transfer to optical plates.

## Rejected sources

- **Gem-trade record pieces (GIA, IGI: 34.59 ct CVD, 10.02 ct HPHT)**: gem stones, not plates; used only as size context, not as window data.
- **Blog and retailer pages on lab-grown diamond prices (S49, S52)**: no method, no primary data; quoted only as flagged context in F5, never in derived figures.
- **makeitfrom.com**: aggregator without primary attribution; superseded by Diamond Materials GmbH and E6 sheets.
- **Wikipedia (Cupola, Pioneer Venus)**: leads only; the Cupola pane thicknesses are flagged secondary.
- **Popular aircraft-window pages (S53)**: qualitative use only.
- **WebFetch summary of Miyazawa (2015) stating 200-300 GPa hardness for sintered C60**: the number exceeds every measured diamond hardness in F2 and could not be checked against the text; kept only as a low-confidence flagged claim.
- **Search-summary units for Plexiglas G (S26: "450,000 MPa", "16,000 MPa")**: unit errors; replaced by the opened Arkema sheet.
- **Handbook Vol 6 Ch 24 (Graphene in Space)**: astrochemistry, not engineering; used only for the list of fullerenes detected in space.
- **NTRS 19950007304 (MCAT 1994)**: unreadable scan.
