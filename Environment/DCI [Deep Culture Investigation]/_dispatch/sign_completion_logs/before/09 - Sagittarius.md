# Avenues of investigation: 09 Sagittarius (belief and philosophy)

**What this file is.** For each Sagittarius concept, every avenue through which a culture's understanding of it can be learned. `Shared/Investigation_Sheet.md` gives the families of angles that apply to every concept; this file makes them specific to each Sagittarius concept. It is written for any culture in the world, with examples drawn mostly from the pilot (Cascadia) and from Spanish and English, because those are the pilot's languages. Examples are prompts for where to look, not findings.

**Marks.** *(check)*: a source, dataset, term or fact given from memory, to be confirmed before use. Indigenous ceremonies, stories and practices are researched only as the nations themselves share them; owned and restricted knowledge is not retold. Every faith community is described from published sources and its own accounts, and never as one voice.

**The concept list** (**not yet settled**; each sign's list is settled with the author just before its pass): faith, meaning, freedom, truth, belief, conviction, travel, honesty, education, fundamentalism. Faith was settled under Sagittarius by the author (2026-11-02). Freedom (also in Aquarius's ten) and travel (also in Gemini's ten) have their proposed homes here; Aquarius and Gemini keep cross-references. No concept in this list has its proposed home in another sign. Questions to settle:
- **Faith and belief:** one entry or two? Written here as two: **faith** is trust in and commitment to what can't be proven; **belief** is holding something to be true, religious or not, and the warrant wanted for it.
- **Belief, conviction and fundamentalism** form a chain. **Conviction** is the firmness of a belief and acting on it. **Fundamentalism** reads as conviction's excess, but it is written as its own entry because it names a movement and a word people use of each other.
- **Conviction** means only a firm belief, the sense Spanish calls *convicción*. The other English sense, a finding of guilt, is not this concept.
- **Truth and honesty:** **truth** is what is so and who is trusted to say it; **honesty** is truthfulness in one's own words and dealings.
- **Freedom and Aries's autonomy and independence:** freedom here is the condition and the ideal (freedom of religion, speech and movement; freedom as a political belief). Deciding for oneself (autonomy) and not depending on others (independence) stay in Aries.
- **Education and Gemini's learning:** education here is formal and higher learning and the standing of the educated; Gemini keeps learning, curiosity and early schooling.
- **Travel and Gemini's short journeys:** long journeys, pilgrimage, migration and the foreign here; commuting and local travel in Gemini. Pilgrimage is placed under travel, with a cross-reference from faith.

## 0. The domain: belief and philosophy

**Traditional areas:** religion, philosophy, higher learning, long journeys. **The domain question:** what is true, and where is meaning found?

- **The religious landscape.** Which faiths are present, and in what shares, down to the county or *municipio*. Canada's census asks religion every ten years (British Columbia had the largest "no religious affiliation" share of any province in 2021 *check*). INEGI's census asks it (Baja California's Catholic share is among Mexico's lowest *check*). The U.S. census does not, so use the U.S. Religion Census (congregations and adherents by county, 2020), Pew's Religious Landscape Study (2007, 2014, 2023-24; by state), PRRI's Census of American Religion (by state and county *check*) and Gallup's state religiosity rankings; ARDA hosts most of these. Regional scholarship: the Pacific Northwest as the "None Zone" (Killen and Silk, eds., 2004, in the Religion by Region series, which has a Pacific volume by Roof and Silk *check*); Stark and Bainbridge's "unchurched belt" in the Pacific states, where they found new religions thriving (*The Future of Religion*, 1985 *check*); irreligion in British Columbia and Washington (Marks, *Infidels and the Damn Churches*, 2017; Block, *The Secular Northwest*, 2016).
- **Religion in public life.** Where faith shows in shared civic life: prayer at council meetings and public ceremonies; religious symbols in public buildings and squares; leaders' talk of God ("God bless America"; the Bible at an inauguration); Sunday as a day of rest, and its fading (shop hours; Sunday sports); whether Canadians keep faith out of politics (a question to research, not a finding); Mexico's *laicidad* as a civic ideal and its anticlerical tradition, with *la Reforma* and the Cristero War as its history; how the churches are seen when they speak on politics.
- **Secularity and unbelief.** Atheist, agnostic and humanist life: humanist associations (the BC Humanist Association; American Humanist Association chapters), secular officiants and funerals, Sunday Assembly congregations (*check*); "spiritual but not religious" (Pisces's spirituality); nature as the sacred (Taylor, *Dark Green Religion*, 2010); Charles Taylor's *A Secular Age* (2007) on belief becoming one option among many.
- **Philosophy and worldviews.** The units' own thinkers: Josiah Royce (born in Grass Valley; *The Philosophy of Loyalty*, 1908, and a history of California, 1886); John Muir's philosophy of wilderness; Alan Watts in Sausalito; Ursula K. Le Guin's version of the *Tao Te Ching* (1997); in Mexico, Vasconcelos (*La raza cósmica*, 1925), Samuel Ramos (*El perfil del hombre y la cultura en México*, 1934), Octavio Paz, Leopoldo Zea and the Hiperión group's "philosophy of the Mexican"; Indigenous philosophies as the nations state them. Present-day Bay Area worldviews, as their adherents and critics publish them: the rationalist community, effective altruism, transhumanism, the Stoicism revival in tech.
- Law is researched only in Libra (the letter of the law) and Capricorn (the spirit of the law).
- **The foreign and the far away.** How the culture looks outward: the Pacific Rim orientation of Vancouver, Seattle and San Francisco; foreign languages spoken and learned (ACS language spoken at home; school foreign-language enrollment); the WVS item "I see myself as a world citizen"; what newcomers are called (Chinook Jargon *cheechako*, a newcomer, against *sourdough*, an old-timer, in the North; *gringo* and *gabacho*; *chilango* for mainland newcomers in the Bajas *check*; Halq'eméylem *Xwelítem*, "hungry people", the Stó:lō word for settlers, as the Stó:lō publish it *check*); exclusion and its memory (the expulsions of Chinese residents from Tacoma, 1885, and Seattle, 1886; the anti-Chinese campaigns in northern Mexico in the 1930s *check*; the head-tax redress movement and Canada's apology, 2006; July 1 kept as "Humiliation Day" by Chinese Canadians after 1923 *check*).
- **Wisdom and who holds it.** Elders and Knowledge Keepers (a term Canadian institutions now use); grandmothers and their *dichos*; clergy; professors and scientists (trust in them: Pew, Gallup); advice columns (Dear Abby began at the *San Francisco Chronicle*, 1956 *check*); therapists and life coaches.
- **Providence, destiny and luck.** Is the future in God's hands, fate's, chance's or one's own? *Si Dios quiere*, *primero Dios*; Manifest Destiny (O'Sullivan, 1845) as a providential reading of the West; the California Dream; optimism (Gallup; WVS). Gambling is under Aries's recklessness.
- **Religious ways** (Fischer's folkway for Sagittarius):
  - church order (congregational, presbyterian, episcopal; the parish; the storefront church; the megachurch, in the Hartford Institute's database *check*) and sacred space (meeting house, mission church, cathedral, longhouse, temple, mosque, synagogue, the roadside *capilla*): how each is built and used;
  - the founders' churches: Yankee Methodist and Congregational missions (Jason Lee, 1834; the Whitmans, 1836-1847); the Franciscan (Alta California, 1769-1823), Jesuit (Loreto, 1697-1767) and Dominican (northern Baja, from 1774) missions; Catholic missions on the Plateau and the Oblates in British Columbia; the Church of England in the colony; Russian Orthodoxy at Sitka and the Orthodox Tlingit communities, as they describe themselves; the Latter-day Saints' colony at San Bernardino (1851-1857);
  - later layers: Pentecostalism born at Azusa Street, Los Angeles (1906); Aimee Semple McPherson's Foursquare Church; the Jesus Movement and Calvary Chapel; Japanese-American Buddhist churches (the Buddhist Churches of America, headquartered in San Francisco); Seattle's Sephardic congregations; Russian sectarians (Doukhobors in British Columbia; Molokans in Los Angeles and in Baja California's Guadalupe Valley, from 1905); Mennonites in the Fraser Valley; evangelical and Pentecostal growth in Baja California; Slavic evangelical refugees around Sacramento and Portland (*check*);
  - Indigenous religious life and its suppression, in the nations' own accounts: the Indian Shaker Church (John Slocum, Squaxin, 1882); the Washat or Seven Drums religion of the Plateau; the Ghost Dances of 1870 and 1889, from the Great Basin; the Bole-Maru among the Pomo and Patwin; the suppression of the potlatch (1884-1951), its hidden continuation and its revival; the Native American Church and peyote;
  - worship style (silent, liturgical, ecstatic); the sabbath; the religious calendar; clergy (training, gender, celibacy, pay); church discipline and shunning; tithing and religious giving; religious schools; religious broadcasting.
- **Freedom ways** (Fischer's folkway for Sagittarius): Fischer found four meanings of freedom in four British founding cultures (ordered, hegemonic, reciprocal and natural freedom; *Albion's Seed*, 1989) and traced the words "liberty" and "freedom" through American history (*Liberty and Freedom*, 2005). The same method asks what each founding population meant by freedom and which institutions carried it. Questions for the pilot: did the Left Coast's Yankee founders bring ordered freedom, and upland-Southern settlers and Dust Bowl migrants natural freedom? How does "peace, order and good government" shape the British Columbia coast? What did Mexican liberal *libertad* (Juárez) and *Tierra y Libertad* (land as freedom; the Magonista rising in Baja California, 1911) leave in the Bajas? What does the Far West mean by freedom from federal control (the Sagebrush Rebellion; the State of Jefferson; the occupation of the Malheur refuge in Harney County, Oregon, 2016)? How does each nation state sovereignty as freedom? The concept itself is section 3.
- **Learning ways** (runner-up; home sign Gemini, `03 - Gemini.md`): here only higher learning and scholarship, in section 9.

## 1. Faith

**Working definition** (settled under Sagittarius by the author, 2026-11-02): trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven: God, gods, spirits, a sacred order; by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.
**Facets:** religious faith (belief in, trust, loyalty, practice); trust in providence; faith that holds through crisis and loss; keeping and breaking faith; faith in institutions, the country, science, the market, technology; finding, losing and changing faith.
**Neighbors and opposites:** belief, trust, hope, devotion, piety, loyalty, confidence; doubt, unbelief, despair, cynicism. **Excess:** credulity, blind faith, fanaticism. **Deficiency:** cynicism, despair, an inability to trust anything unseen.

- **Words:** "faith", "faithful", "people of faith", "faith-based", "keep the faith", "leap of faith", "have faith", "blessed", "Lord willing", "God's plan", "born again", "backslider", "lapsed", "devout"; Spanish *fe*, *tener fe*, *creyente*, *los fieles*, *devoto*, *primero Dios* (Mexican: "God first", added to any plan), *si Dios quiere*, *Dios mediante*, *ojalá* (from Arabic: "may God will it"), *Dios proveerá*, *encomendarse a Dios*, *la fe mueve montañas*, *la fe del carbonero* (the charcoal-burner's faith: simple and unquestioning, a phrase that marks the line between faith and credulity), *manda* (a vow to a saint, paid with a pilgrimage or a sacrifice), *milagritos* and *exvotos* (votive charms and paintings that thank a saint for a favor). Chinook Jargon *Saghalie Tyee*, God, "the chief above" (*saxali tayi* in the Grand Ronde dictionary, 2012 *check*). "The Creator" in many Indigenous speakers' English; Indigenous-language terms only as the nations' language programs publish them.
- **Beliefs and standing:** is faith a gift (grace), a choice, a duty, an inheritance or a weakness? Faith against works (Protestant and Catholic teaching); faith and reason (the encyclical *Fides et Ratio*, 1998); private faith against public faith; American civil religion (Bellah, 1967); whether one must believe in God to be moral (Pew's global surveys, 2019-2020 *check*); folk theory: is faith born, taught, caught in a crisis, or given by God?
- **Feeling and the body:** kneeling, crossing oneself (*persignarse*), raised hands, speaking in tongues (Pentecostal worship since Azusa Street), fasting, walking or crawling the last stretch of a pilgrimage, touching images, lighting candles; the felt assurance of being saved; faith in illness: prayer for healing, faith healing (Bethel Church in Redding), religious coping (Pargament's RCOPE scale); faith worn on the body (crosses, medals of the Virgin, scapulars, saints as tattoos, head coverings, Ash Wednesday's ashes).
- **Everyday practice:** grace at meals; daily prayer; home altars (*altarcitos*); saints on dashboards; church attendance; Bible reading and study groups; tithing; prayer chains and prayer requests; Christian radio; *mandas* and their payment; blessings of homes, cars and animals (the Blessing of the Animals on Olvera Street, Los Angeles *check*); whether faith is talked about at work.
- **Life course:** baptism; the *presentación* of a three-year-old in church (*check*); first communion and confirmation; bar and bat mitzvah; Sunday school, youth groups and Bible camps; religious schools; adult conversion and "rededication"; faith in old age; last rites; how and when children are told about God.
- **Ritual:** Christmas and Easter; *Semana Santa* processions and Passion plays; Las Posadas; December 12 for the Virgin of Guadalupe (the procession in East Los Angeles *check*); patron-saint *fiestas* (Loreto; La Paz *check*); Ramadan and Eid; the High Holy Days; Obon at Japanese-American temples; revivals; Indigenous ceremonies only as the nations share them.
- **Institutions:** parishes and dioceses (the Archdiocese of Los Angeles, the largest in the U.S. by Catholic population *check*), denominations, congregations, temples, mosques, synagogues; faith-based charities (Catholic Charities; World Vision, based in Federal Way, Washington); chaplaincies in hospitals, universities and the military; religious orders (Jesuits, Franciscans, Dominicans) and the schools and hospitals they run; interfaith councils; Christian radio stations and publishers.
- **Rewards and sanctions:** respect for the devout, or mockery: "holy roller", "Jesus freak", "Bible-thumper"; Mexican *mocho* (a sanctimonious, conservative Catholic), *beata*, *aleluya* (for evangelicals *check*); family pressure and estrangement over leaving or changing faith; distrust of nonbelievers (see belief); discrimination people say they have felt over their religion (Pew's surveys; INEGI's ENADIS).
- **Expression:** hymns, gospel and *alabanzas*; contemporary Christian music (Maranatha! Music, from Calvary Chapel, 1971 *check*; Bethel Music, Redding); sermons and testimonies; *retablos* and mission art; Richard Rodriguez, *Darling: A Spiritual Autobiography* (2013); Anne Lamott, *Traveling Mercies* (1999, Marin County); religious film and media; Indigenous stories as the nations share them.
- **Exemplars:** saints and missionaries (Junípero Serra, canonized 2015, a contested figure); Saint Toribio Romo, venerated by migrants (*check*); Aimee Semple McPherson; César Chávez's Catholic faith; prophets of Indigenous religious movements, as their communities recount them; counter-exemplars: frauds and abusers in the pulpit.
- **History:** the missions and what they cost Native peoples (the nations' accounts first); church-run residential and boarding schools; the suppression of the potlatch; the Cristero War (1926-1929); Azusa Street (1906) and the Pentecostal spread; the Jesus Movement; secularization in the Northwest; the clergy-abuse crisis and what it did to trust in the churches (the dioceses of Portland, Spokane, San Diego, Oakland, San Francisco, Santa Rosa and others *check*); the Tk'emlúps te Secwépemc announcement at Kamloops (2021) and the church fires that followed.
- **Measures:** the importance of religion, prayer, belief in God and attendance (Pew Religious Landscape Study by state; Gallup by state; GSS); the WVS item "How important is God in your life?" (1 to 10) and its practice items; the ISSP Religion modules (1991, 1998, 2008, 2018); Statistics Canada's religiosity index from the General Social Survey; INEGI's census and Mexico's national survey of religious beliefs and practices (ENCREER, 2016 *check*); Pew's *Religion in Latin America* (2014); Latinobarómetro's religion items; confidence in churches (Gallup; WVS).
- **Variation:** denomination; immigrant congregations (Korean, Filipino, Chinese, Latino, Slavic); Indigenous Christians and traditionalists, each nation on its own terms; urban and rural; gender; generation (the young nones); the Northwest's cities against the Central Valley, Redding or the Fraser Valley (measured, not assumed).
- **Relations:** belief, meaning, conviction, fundamentalism, travel (pilgrimage); trust (Scorpio); hope (Aquarius); spirituality and surrender (Pisces); death and rebirth (Scorpio).
- **Who it applies to:** faith in institutions (Gallup's confidence series), science, the market, the nation, technology; whether other faiths are seen as true (Pew: "many religions can lead to eternal life"); whether people think a machine could have faith, and how they feel about AI in religious roles such as writing sermons or praying with people (present-day attitudes; surveys *check*).
- **Silences:** clergy's doubts; quiet leaving ("deconstruction"); abuse kept hidden; mixed-faith families; faith kept hidden at secular workplaces.
- **Change:** the rise of the nones; deconstruction; Catholic decline and evangelical growth in Mexico (INEGI's censuses, 1970-2020); online worship after COVID-19; church closures; Indigenous ceremonial revival.

**Key questions:** Is faith a gift, a choice or an inheritance, and is lacking it a lack or a freedom? Besides God, where does the culture place its faith?

## 2. Meaning

**Working definition:** what makes life, work and suffering worth it: the sense that life has a point, and where that point is found.
**Facets:** the meaning of life (cosmic) and meaning in life (personal); the three parts psychologists name: coherence, purpose and significance (Martela and Steger, 2016); the sources (family, faith, work, nature, service, art, causes, the nation); meaning in suffering; calling and vocation; legacy; losing meaning (anomie, despair).
**Neighbors and opposites:** purpose, calling, vocation, significance, fulfillment; emptiness, absurdity, nihilism, anomie (Durkheim), despair. **Excess:** grandiosity; reading everything as a sign. **Deficiency:** emptiness, drift.

- **Words:** "the meaning of life", "purpose", "calling", "vocation", "make a difference", "everything happens for a reason", "follow your passion", "find yourself", "what's the point", "bucket list", "legacy", "purpose-driven"; Spanish *el sentido de la vida*, *propósito*, *vocación*, *razón de ser*, *¿para qué?*, *todo pasa por algo*, *vale la pena*, *ganas de vivir*, *llenar un vacío*, *nini* (a young person who neither studies nor works: a word charged with drift); borrowed words reshaped by self-help (*ikigai* as Western books use it, against its Japanese use *check*).
- **Beliefs and standing:** is meaning given (by God, tradition, nature) or made (by choice, work, love)? Is it found in the self, the family, the work or the cause? Is suffering meaningful (redemptive suffering in Catholic teaching; "offering it up", *ofrecerlo a Dios*) or only something to end? The Calvinist calling against the therapeutic gospel of self-fulfillment (Rieff, *The Triumph of the Therapeutic*, 1966; Bellah and others, *Habits of the Heart*, 1985, with its "Sheilaism", a religion of one); Frankl's *Man's Search for Meaning* as a popular text; Indigenous teachings that place meaning in relations with land, kin and ancestors, as the nations state them.
- **Feeling and the body:** awe (Keltner's research at Berkeley); flow; depression and despair; "deaths of despair" (Case and Deaton, 2020); wilderness as a source of meaning; psychedelic experience as meaning-seeking (guided psilocybin sessions; retreats).
- **Everyday practice:** what people do to make life mean something: family time, worship, volunteering, careers chosen for "impact", outdoor life, art, journaling, therapy, retreats; companies' mission statements and "purpose" branding; obituaries and what they praise.
- **Life course:** "What do you want to be?"; career counseling; graduation speeches; the midlife crisis; retirement and the loss of a role; life review in old age (Erikson's integrity against despair); meaning at the end of life (hospice spiritual care; the reasons patients give in Oregon's yearly reports on medical aid in dying *check*).
- **Ritual:** memorials and anniversaries; Día de Muertos as remembrance (death ways are Scorpio's); New Year's resolutions; retreats; vision quests and fasts only as the nations share them, and the sanction on outsiders who copy them.
- **Institutions:** churches; counseling and logotherapy; hospice chaplaincy; life coaching; university courses on purpose (Stanford's "Designing Your Life" *check*); the human potential movement's centers (Esalen, Big Sur, 1962; est, San Francisco, 1971); Oregon's psilocybin service centers (from 2023); Indigenous healing and culture programs.
- **Rewards and sanctions:** admiration for people who "found their calling"; contempt for drift (the "slacker", the *nini*); the shame of a pointless job (Graeber, *Bullshit Jobs*, 2018); suspicion of people who seek meaning in odd places (cults, self-styled prophets; see fundamentalism).
- **Expression:** *The Purpose Driven Life* (Rick Warren, Saddleback Church, Orange County, 2002); Joseph Campbell's "follow your bliss"; Steve Jobs's Stanford commencement address (2005); Octavio Paz, *El laberinto de la soledad* (1950); Steinbeck; Le Guin; songs; TED talks.
- **Exemplars:** John Muir (meaning in wilderness); César Chávez and Dolores Huerta (lives given to a cause); saints and healers; people who left wealth for service; counter-exemplars: the burned-out, the cynic, the rich man with nothing to live for.
- **History:** the Puritan calling and Yankee reform; the Gold Rush's chase after fortune; the Bay Area counterculture and the human potential movement; the Jesus Movement; tech's "change the world"; Indigenous meaning broken by dispossession and rebuilt through cultural revival, in the nations' own words.
- **Measures:** Pew's open-ended "Where Americans find meaning in life" (2018) and its 17-economy study with Canada (2021) (*check*); the WVS item "How often do you think about the meaning and purpose of life?"; life satisfaction (WVS; BRFSS by state; Gallup); Statistics Canada's "life has meaning and purpose" item in its quality-of-life surveys (*check*); INEGI's self-reported well-being survey (BIARE), with eudaimonic items, by state (*check*); the Meaning in Life Questionnaire (Steger) in regional studies; deaths of despair by county (CDC WONDER: suicide, overdose, alcohol).
- **Variation:** class (meaning from work or from family); religiosity; generation; gender; immigrants; Indigenous; tech workers and farmworkers.
- **Relations:** faith, belief, conviction; hope and idealism (Aquarius); work and usefulness (Virgo); creativity and joy (Leo); death (Scorpio); escapism and spirituality (Pisces).
- **Who it applies to:** whether nature, animals and the land have meaning in themselves; "purpose-driven" companies; whether people fear automation and AI will take the meaning out of work, or expect machines to help them find it (present-day surveys *check*).
- **Silences:** despair and suicide; men's loneliness; emptiness among the comfortable; talk of death.
- **Change:** the decline of institutional sources of meaning; the "great resignation"; the wellness industry; psychedelic therapy; remote work.

**Key questions:** Is meaning given or made? Where does the culture look for it first: God, family, work, cause, nature or the self? Is suffering meaningful?

## 3. Freedom

**Working definition:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal. Proposed home Sagittarius; also in Aquarius's ten. Deciding for oneself (autonomy) and not depending on others (independence) stay in Aries.
**Facets:** freedom from and freedom to (negative and positive liberty; Berlin, 1958); freedoms of religion, conscience, speech, press, assembly and movement; economic freedom; freedom from want and fear (Roosevelt's Four Freedoms, 1941); collective freedom (independence, sovereignty, liberation); release (from slavery, captivity, addiction, debt); inner and spiritual freedom; the freedom of open land and the road; academic freedom.
**Neighbors and opposites:** liberty, emancipation, liberation, sovereignty, license; bondage, slavery, captivity, oppression, tyranny, confinement. **Excess:** license, disorder. **Deficiency:** servility, oppression.

- **Words:** "freedom" and "liberty" (Fischer traces them to different roots: Latin *libertas*, and a Germanic word tied to "dear" and "friend", belonging to a free people; *Liberty and Freedom*, 2005 *check*); "free country", "land of the free", "freedom isn't free", "don't tread on me", "live free", "free speech", "free spirit", "free-range", "it's my right"; "the True North strong and free" ("O Canada"); "peace, order and good government", the phrase Canadians often set against "life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness"; Spanish *libertad*, *libre*, *libertinaje* (license: Spanish gives the excess its own word), *libre albedrío*, *libertad de culto*, *libertad de expresión*, *Tierra y Libertad*, *libertador*, *no tener dueño* (to have no master). Indigenous terms only as the nations publish them.
- **Beliefs and standing:** which freedom ranks first: from government, from want, or of conscience? When freedom meets equality, security or order, which wins (the WVS items under Measures)? Christian freedom (from sin; freedom in Christ); liberal, libertarian and anarchist thought (Flores Magón's anarchism; the libertarian institutions of Los Angeles and the Bay Area); Mexican liberalism (Juárez); freedom bound to responsibility, kin and land in Indigenous teachings, as the nations state them; is freedom natural, a privilege, or won?
- **Feeling and the body:** the open road, open space and the outdoors; the body in confinement; nudity norms (Wreck Beach, Vancouver; Black's Beach, San Diego); drugs and altered states, felt as freedom or as bondage (cannabis culture; psychedelics; addiction as unfreedom); masks and vaccines in the pandemic, felt as care or as constraint. Bodily autonomy is under Aries.
- **Everyday practice:** speaking one's mind; carrying a gun as a freedom; the car, the RV and "van life"; camping on public land; homeschooling; protest; changing religion; living openly gay (San Francisco's Castro); crossing borders.
- **Life course:** teenage freedoms (the driver's license, curfews, "free-range kids"); leaving home; retirement as freedom ("Freedom 55", a Canadian insurance slogan *check*); freedom lost in old age (care homes, the lost license); release after confinement (leaving a strict home, a high-control church, rehab or the military).
- **Ritual:** the Fourth of July; Canada Day; the *Grito* on the night of September 15; Juneteenth; Pride; May Day; protest marches; anniversaries of liberation (the end of internment; emancipation).
- **Institutions:** civil-liberties associations (ACLU affiliates; the BC Civil Liberties Association, 1962); libertarian institutions (the Reason Foundation; the Independent Institute; the Fraser Institute; the Cato Institute's first years in San Francisco *check*); libraries and their reading-freedom campaigns (Banned Books Week, from the American Library Association; Canada's Freedom to Read Week *check*); museums that keep the memory of freedom lost (the Japanese American National Museum, Los Angeles; the Nikkei National Museum, Burnaby *check*); press clubs and journalists' associations; Pride organizations; the agencies that keep public land open to roam (the U.S. Forest Service; the Bureau of Land Management; Recreation Sites and Trails BC *check*).
- **Rewards and sanctions:** freedom fighters honored; *libertinaje* condemned; how offensive speech is met socially (shunning, boycotts, deplatforming), and whether it is borne as the price of freedom (Pew's global survey on free expression, 2015 *check*); disputes over "cancel culture"; the social sanction on those who "abuse" freedom.
- **Expression:** anthems; Norman Rockwell's *Four Freedoms*; Woody Guthrie; protest songs; *corridos* of the Revolution; Chicano murals; art on the border fence at Tijuana; road films (*Easy Rider*); flags (the Gadsden flag) and mottos; Chief Joseph's speech in Washington, D.C. (1879), asking to be "a free man".
- **Exemplars:** Chief Joseph (Nez Perce); Biddy Mason (born enslaved; free in Los Angeles from 1856, she became a landowner and benefactor); Fred Korematsu; Mario Savio; Harvey Milk; Ricardo Flores Magón; Benito Juárez; Emiliano Zapata; contested ones: the Malheur occupiers; the leaders of the Freedom Convoy (2022).
- **History:** freedom for whom: Black exclusion in early Oregon and the sundown towns it left *check*; California, a free state (1850) where Native people were held in forced labor in the same years; the anti-Chinese movement (the Workingmen's Party, San Francisco, 1877; the expulsions from Tacoma, 1885, and Seattle, 1886); the internment of Japanese Americans and Japanese Canadians (1942); the IWW free-speech fights (Spokane, 1909; Fresno; San Diego, 1912); the Free Speech Movement (Berkeley, 1964); gay liberation; the Magonista rising (1911); the Cristero War (freedom of worship); the fish wars and fish-ins of the 1960s and 1970s, a fight for the freedom to fish where the nations always had; congregations that kept meeting through the COVID-19 closures (2020-2021) and the protests around them *check*.
- **Measures:** the WVS items on freedom against equality and freedom against security (*check*); the Gallup World Poll's satisfaction with the freedom to choose what to do with one's life (country); Freedom House's *Freedom in the World* and the press-freedom indexes (RSF; country); Fraser's *Economic Freedom of North America* (practitioner; declared orientation); the liberty foundation in moral-foundations research (Haidt, *The Righteous Mind*, 2012); FIRE's campus free-speech rankings (practitioner) and the Knight Foundation and Gallup surveys of students; support for free expression (Latinobarómetro; AmericasBarometer); self-censorship, the share who say they keep their views to themselves (Cato and YouGov, 2020 *check*).

## 9. Education

**Working definition:** formal and higher learning (schools, universities, degrees, scholarship and lifelong study) and the standing of the educated person. Gemini keeps learning, curiosity and early schooling.
**Facets:** higher education; professional and vocational training; religious education and seminaries; Indigenous education and language programs; adult and lifelong learning; credentials against knowledge ("book smart" and "street smart"; field-tested knowledge); access and inequality; what education is for (citizenship, a job, character, faith, liberation).
**Neighbors and opposites:** schooling, scholarship, knowledge, wisdom, cultivation; ignorance, illiteracy, anti-intellectualism. **Excess:** credentialism, elitism, pedantry. **Deficiency:** ignorance, anti-intellectualism.

- **Words:** "education", "college", "higher ed", "degree", "the trades", "book smart", "street smart", "egghead", "ivory tower", "elite", "first-gen", "college material", "self-taught", "the school of hard knocks"; Spanish *educación* (*bien educado* means well-mannered: Spanish puts manners inside education), *preparación* (*una persona preparada*, an educated person), *estudios*, *carrera*, *título*, *licenciado* (*Lic.*, a title of address for anyone with a degree; likewise *ingeniero* and *doctor*), *maestro*, *normalista* (a teacher trained at an *escuela normal*), *prepa*, *cédula profesional*, *la letra con sangre entra* (an old saying: learning goes in with blood); Chinook Jargon *kumtux*, to know (*munk-kemteks*, to teach, in the title of the Grand Ronde dictionary *check*).
- **Beliefs and standing:** is education a right, a public good, a private investment, a path to God, or liberation (Freire, *Pedagogy of the Oppressed*)? College for all, or the trades? Does higher learning threaten faith? Is the educated person respected or suspected? Education as manners and morals (*educación*); Indigenous understandings of education as a relation to land, language and elders, as the nations state them.
- **Feeling and the body:** test anxiety; the impostor feeling of first-generation students; pride at graduation; the accent and dress of the educated; corporal punishment in schools and its end (the paddle; the strap in Canadian schools *check*).
- **Everyday practice:** homework help; tutoring; test prep; college-application culture; adult classes; libraries and public lectures; reading; online courses; families' sacrifices for tuition (*colegiaturas*); scholarships; student debt; heritage-language Saturday schools.
- **Life course:** admission; graduation (*graduación* with its Mass, *padrinos* and gown); being "first in the family"; returning to school; graduate degrees; elders learning their language.

- **Institutions:** the California Master Plan for Higher Education (1960: the University of California, the California State University and the community colleges); Stanford, Caltech, the University of Washington, the University of Oregon, UBC, Simon Fraser, the University of Victoria; UABC (1957), UABCS (*check*), El Colegio de la Frontera Norte and CICESE (Ensenada, 1973); tribal colleges (Northwest Indian College, Lummi *check*; D-Q University, Davis, 1971-2005); Indigenous-language teacher certification (Washington's First Peoples' Language, Culture, and Oral Tribal Traditions certificate *check*); seminaries (Fuller in Pasadena; Regent College in Vancouver; Mount Angel in Oregon); Mexico's SEP (1921) and the free textbooks (from 1959; the 2023 dispute over new ones); teachers' unions (the California Teachers Association; SNTE and CNTE); student aid (federal loans; StudentAid BC; Mexico's Benito Juárez scholarships).

- **Expression:** university mottos (the University of California's *Fiat lux*; Stanford's *Die Luft der Freiheit weht*, "the wind of freedom blows"; UBC's *Tuum est*); *Stand and Deliver* (1988); campus novels; commencement speeches; *corridos* and films about migrant students.
- **Exemplars:** Jaime Escalante (Garfield High School, East Los Angeles); José Vasconcelos and the rural teachers of the 1920s *misiones culturales*; Vi Hilbert (Upper Skagit), who taught Lushootseed; Ed Roberts at Berkeley (1962); counter-exemplars: frauds and diploma sellers.

## 10. Fundamentalism

**Working definition:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore it and defend it against modern change. Whether the word fits secular and political certainties is itself contested.
**Facets:** scriptural literalism and inerrancy; separation from "the world"; purity and boundary-keeping; restoring a lost original; the authority of one text or one leader; high-control groups; militancy (rare); fundamentalist currents in many faiths (the Fundamentalism Project's comparison); the word as an insult.
**Neighbors and opposites:** orthodoxy, traditionalism, literalism, dogmatism, integralism, zealotry, extremism; modernism, liberalism, pluralism, ecumenism. **Excess:** fanaticism, coercion, violence. **The other side:** relativism and syncretism; from inside the movement, any compromise with the world.

- **Words:** "fundamentalist" (coined in 1920 by the Baptist editor Curtis Lee Laws, from *The Fundamentals*, 1910-1915), "fundie" (pejorative), "Bible-believing", "inerrancy", "biblical worldview", "Bible-thumper", "cult", "brainwashed", "true believer", "extremist", "purity culture", "exvangelical"; "the Bible Belt of BC" (the Fraser Valley); Spanish *fundamentalista*, *integrista*, *fanático*, *mocho*, *santurrón*, *secta* (in Mexican Catholic usage, any non-Catholic group: read it for who is being marked), *aleluya* (for evangelicals *check*), *cristero*, *sinarquista*.
- **Beliefs and standing:** is holding to the letter fidelity or rigidity? Who calls whom fundamentalist, and does anyone claim the word? Inerrancy and literalism; the fundamentalist-modernist controversy (Marsden, *Fundamentalism and American Culture*, 1980); comparisons across faiths (Marty and Appleby, eds., *The Fundamentalism Project*, 1991-1995; Almond, Appleby and Sivan, *Strong Religion*, 2003); Catholic integralism and Mexican *sinarquismo*; "fundamentalism" as a charge in political speech (market, environmental, secular).
- **Feeling and the body:** purity culture (abstinence pledges; modest dress); certainty and fear of the world; ecstatic worship or strict restraint; leaving and its aftermath ("religious trauma", a contested term); the Sons of Freedom's nude protests in British Columbia.
- **Everyday practice:** homeschooling and Christian schools; avoiding secular media; dress codes; courtship rules; creation museums (the Institute for Creation Research began near San Diego in 1970 and moved to Dallas *check*); Bible camps and prophecy conferences; faith healing (Bethel, Redding); separatist settlements.
- **Life course:** believers' baptism; purity pledges; children raised in high-control groups, and what happens when they leave; adult converts; shunning and excommunication.
- **Ritual:** revivals and altar calls; crusades (Billy Graham's 1949 Los Angeles crusade made his name); mass ocean baptisms (Calvary Chapel at Pirate's Cove, Corona del Mar, 1970s); Bible and prophecy conferences.
- **Institutions:** the Bible Institute of Los Angeles (Biola, 1908; co-founded by the Union Oil magnate Lyman Stewart, who also paid for *The Fundamentals*); Fuller Theological Seminary (1947, founded as a "new evangelical" break from separatist fundamentalism); The Master's University and Grace Community Church (Sun Valley, Los Angeles; defied COVID-19 orders in 2020); Calvary Chapel; Trinity Western University; Christian homeschool associations; the fundamentalist Mormon community at Bountiful, British Columbia; Mormon colonies in Chihuahua and Sonora (from the 1880s); La Luz del Mundo, based in Guadalajara, with large congregations in California; deprogramming (Ted Patrick, San Diego, from the 1970s) and the anti-cult movement; extremism monitors (SPLC, ADL: advocacy groups, tagged practitioner).
- **Rewards and sanctions:** inside, praise for steadfastness and the shunning of leavers; outside, mockery ("fundie", *mocho*) and lost jobs; British Columbia's seizure of Doukhobor children, held at New Denver (1953-1959), and the province's later statement of regret *check*; anti-cult hostility toward small faiths.
- **Expression:** radio preaching (Charles Fuller's *Old Fashioned Revival Hour*, broadcast from Long Beach *check*); Jack Chick's tracts (Chick Publications, Rancho Cucamonga); Hal Lindsey's *The Late Great Planet Earth* (1970); the *Left Behind* novels (co-written by Tim LaHaye, a San Diego pastor); ex-members' memoirs; documentaries (*Jesus Camp*, 2006).
- **Exemplars:** Lyman Stewart; R. A. Torrey; Charles Fuller; Henry Morris; Tim LaHaye; contested: Aimee Semple McPherson, Chuck Smith, John MacArthur, the Cristeros (some beatified); counter-exemplars: Jim Jones (Peoples Temple, from Redwood Valley and San Francisco to Jonestown, 1978); Heaven's Gate (Rancho Santa Fe, 1997).
- **History:** the 1920s controversy, with Los Angeles one of its centers; the Scopes trial in national memory; Fuller's 1947 break; the Jesus Movement; the religious right's California campaigns (against gay teachers, 1978; against same-sex marriage, 2008); Peoples Temple; Bountiful; the Doukhobor Sons of Freedom (bombings and arsons, 1950s-1960s); the Cristero War and the *sinarquista* colony in Baja California Sur (1940s *check*); Pentecostal growth in Mexico.
- **Measures:** biblical literalism (Gallup's trend; GSS; Pew Religious Landscape Study by state *check*); creationism (Gallup; Pew); "my religion is the one true faith" against "many religions can lead to eternal life" (Pew RLS by state); Altemeyer and Hunsberger's Religious Fundamentalism Scale (1992; revised 2004) and Altemeyer's right-wing authoritarianism scale, in regional samples; Christian nationalism by state (PRRI *check*); evangelical adherents by county (U.S. Religion Census); homeschooling rates; INEGI's counts of evangelical and other non-Catholic Christians by *municipio*; religious discrimination (INEGI's ENADIS, 2017 and 2022 *check*).
- **Variation:** within each denomination; generation (exvangelicals); region (the Central Valley, Redding and the Fraser Valley against the cities: measured, not assumed); immigrant churches (Latino Pentecostals; Slavic evangelicals); Indigenous Christians and traditionalists; party (Christian nationalism).
- **Relations:** conviction (its excess), faith, belief, truth (the one truth), education (creationism; homeschooling), freedom (religious-liberty claims); authority and tradition (Capricorn); control and power (Scorpio); surrender and fear (Pisces); nonconformity (Aquarius).
- **Who it applies to:** secular movements accused of it; women's roles (complementarianism); children in high-control groups; outsiders as targets of mission; machines: religious statements on AI (the Southern Baptist Convention's 2019 statement; the Vatican's *Antiqua et Nova*, 2025) and a church founded in California to worship AI (Way of the Future, 2017-2021) (present-day attitudes).
- **Silences:** abuse inside high-control groups; leavers' stories; the fundamentalist roots of mainstream institutions; doubt inside the movement.
- **Change:** deconstruction and the exvangelical movement; Christian nationalism; Pentecostal growth among Latinos and in Mexico; online radicalization; "fundamentalist" as a political insult.

**Key questions:** Is holding to the letter fidelity or fanaticism? Who is called fundamentalist, by whom, and does anyone claim the word?
