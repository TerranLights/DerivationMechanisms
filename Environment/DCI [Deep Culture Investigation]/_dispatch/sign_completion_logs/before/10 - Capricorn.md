# Avenues of investigation: 10 Capricorn (authority and duty)

**What this file is.** For each Capricorn concept, every avenue through which a culture's understanding of it can be learned. `Shared/Investigation_Sheet.md` gives the families of angles that apply to every concept; this file makes them specific to each Capricorn concept. It is written for any culture in the world, with examples drawn mostly from the pilot (Cascadia) and from Spanish and English, because those are the pilot's languages. Examples are prompts for where to look, not findings.

**Marks.** *(check)*: a source, dataset, term or fact given from memory, to be confirmed before use. Indigenous ceremonies, stories and practices are researched only as the nations themselves share them; owned and restricted knowledge is not retold. **Law** is treated here as a cultural concept: what law means to people and how they relate to it. The formal legal system is one of its avenues; the system itself is profiled in Stage 1 (`Templates/T1_Lineage_Profiles.md`, profiles 01 and 02).

**The concept list** (**not yet settled**; it is settled with the author just before this sign's pass): authority, duty, responsibility, discipline, ambition, hierarchy, merit, law, order. Duty was settled under Capricorn by the author (2026-11-02). Questions for the author:
- **Recognition** is in Capricorn's ten too; its proposed home is Leo (cross-reference at the end).
- **Order** is in Virgo's ten too; its proposed home is Capricorn, with Virgo keeping a cross-reference. Order is also one of this sign's folkways (order ways); section 0 covers the folkway's own angle and points to section 9.
- **Discipline** is in a near-synonym group with self-discipline and self-control. This file researches all three under discipline: discipline imposed by others and discipline of oneself (as Aries researches self-reliance under independence).
- **Duty and responsibility** overlap, and Spanish splits the ground differently (*deber*, *obligación*, *cumplir*, *responsabilidad*). This file treats duty as what is owed, to whom and by what tie, and responsibility as answering for outcomes: taking charge, being accountable, bearing blame. Should they stay two entries?
- **Law** also sits in Sagittarius's traditional areas (`Shared/Investigation_Sheet.md`, Part C), and it borders justice (Libra) and crime (Pisces).
- **Hierarchy** overlaps rank ways, and **authority** overlaps power ways (both folkways here) and power and control (Scorpio).
- **Possibly missing from the list:** status, reputation and career (the sign's traditional areas, covered in section 0); obedience and deference; tradition (the Aries file points to Capricorn for it); legitimacy; loyalty; patience and the deferred reward; seniority.

## 0. The domain: authority and duty

**Traditional areas:** career, status, reputation, authority, responsibility. **The domain question:** who commands, who obeys, and what is owed?

- **Where authority comes from.** Weber's three kinds of legitimate rule: traditional, charismatic and legal-rational ("Politics as a Vocation", 1919; *Economy and Society*); Arendt's "What Is Authority?" (in *Between Past and Future*, 1961), on authority as neither force nor persuasion; the Roman split between *auctoritas* and *potestas*. For each culture: which sources people accept (age, birth, God, election, expertise, wealth, office, the land, the ancestors) and which they suspect.
- **Distance between ranks.** Hofstede's power distance; GLOBE's power distance, asked twice, as things are and as they should be (the gap is evidence too); Trompenaars's achieved against ascribed status, and universal rules against personal ties; tightness and looseness (Gelfand et al., 2011, countries; Harrington and Gelfand, 2014, U.S. states); the authority foundation of Moral Foundations Theory (Graham, Haidt and Nosek).
- **Political cultures.** Elazar's moralistic, individualistic and traditionalistic cultures (by state and within states); Woodard's Left Coast, Far West and El Norte (*American Nations*, 2011); Lipset's claim that Canadians defer to authority more than Americans (*Continental Divide*, 1990), against Michael Adams's Environics data arguing the reverse (*Fire and Ice*, 2003): a disagreement to record, not settle; Canada's "peace, order and good government" (Constitution Act, 1867, s. 91) set beside "life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness".
- **The culture's own interpreters.** Mexican writing on authority, the family and the state: Octavio Paz (*El laberinto de la soledad*, 1950); Rogelio Díaz-Guerrero's "historic-sociocultural premises" of the Mexican family, such as the father's unquestioned authority and the mother's self-sacrifice, measured with scales from the 1950s on and contested since (*Psicología del mexicano*, *check* edition); Roger Bartra's critique of national-character writing (*La jaula de la melancolía*, 1987); Larissa Adler de Lomnitz on favors, *compadrazgo* and connections inside formal institutions (*check* titles). Western historians on authority in the U.S. West: Patricia Limerick, Richard White, Donald Worster (*Rivers of Empire*, 1985, on power through water). Native nations' own statements of governance, law and responsibility, as they publish them.
- **The culture's own map of the domain.** Which words it joins or splits: Spanish *derecho* is both "law" and "a right", where English splits them; *deber* is both "duty" and "must"; *cumplir* (to fulfill what is owed) has no single English match. Which of this sign's concepts the culture treats as central, and which it hardly names.
- **Outsiders' guides.** Business-culture guides on hierarchy, deciding and scheduling (Erin Meyer, *The Culture Map*, 2014); immigrant orientation guides; military cultural handbooks; guides for Americans working in Mexico and Mexicans working in the U.S. and Canada (*check* titles). Tag them practitioner; they record what newcomers are told to expect.

  - measures: occupation by county (ACS); job tenure (BLS supplement); the WVS item on how important work is; INEGI's ENOE; Statistics Canada's Labour Force Survey.
- **Status** (traditional area):
  - what confers standing: money, education, title, surname, skin color, land, old family, office, giving, piety, skill; inverted displays (the founder's hoodie; "stealth wealth"); Veblen's conspicuous consumption (1899); Fussell, *Class* (1983); Bourdieu, *Distinction* (1979);

  - measures: the MacArthur ladder of subjective social status (Adler et al., 2000); GSS subjective class; occupational prestige scores (GSS 1989 and 2012 *check*; Treiman's international scale; Pineo and Porter for Canada *check*); skin color and attainment (INEGI's 2016 intergenerational mobility module with its color palette; Telles, *Pigmentocracies*, 2014 *check*).

  - machines and patrons: city machines and the Progressive reforms (California, 1911); *caciques*; *acarreados* (people bused to rallies); *corporativismo*; captured union leadership (*charrismo sindical*);

## 1. Authority

**Working definition:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.
**Facets:** parental, religious, political, legal, workplace, military and police, professional and expert (doctors, scientists, teachers), traditional (elders, hereditary chiefs) and charismatic authority; office against informal standing; authority over the body, the household and the land; obedience and its limits; questioning and resisting authority.
**Neighbors and opposites:** legitimacy, command, power, leadership, respect, deference, obedience; anarchy, rebellion, defiance, insubordination. **Excess:** authoritarianism, tyranny, abuse of office, blind obedience. **Deficiency:** a vacuum of authority, permissiveness.


**The authoritarian-libertarian axis lives here** (author, 2026-11-10): how far authority reaches into daily life, and how hard it enforces its rules.
- **The permission share** (the headline): of a fixed list of everyday activities, the share that is forbidden or needs permission. 0% is the far libertarian end, 100% the far authoritarian end (`Shared/Permission_Share_List.md`). It is scored by reading the law in force, as Libra's letter-of-the-law research does.
- **The correctional-control share** (its companion): adults in prison, in jail, on probation or on parole, as a share of all adults. It also rises with crime, so it stands beside the headline rather than replacing it.
- **Two figures,** as for the left-right measure: the government's own level, and all levels combined.
- **The numbers** are collected at Stage 2 (`Shared/Sources_and_Datasets.md`, section H). Here they are read beside everything else the culture shows about authority: whom people obey, how much permission they expect to need, and how they feel about being watched and ruled.
- **Words:** "authority", "the authorities", "the powers that be", "the Man", "the brass", "higher-ups", "boss", "bossy", "chain of command", "pull rank", "go over someone's head", "because I said so", "who died and made you king?", "let me speak to your manager", "Question Authority" (a 1970s bumper sticker), "sir" and "ma'am"; "tyee", Pacific Northwest English from Chinuk Wawa for a chief or boss, and also a big Chinook salmon (the Grand Ronde Tribes' Chinuk Wawa dictionary, 2012); Spanish *autoridad*, *las autoridades*, *el que manda*, *mandón*, *el mero mero*, *el patrón*, *el jefe* and *la jefa* (in Mexican slang also one's father and mother), *¿mande?* (the Mexican polite answer when called, literally "command [me]"; its colonial origin is a popular claim *check*), *a sus órdenes*, *cacique*, *caudillo*, *tlatoani* (a Nahuatl word for ruler, used ironically of Mexican presidents *check*), *respeto*, *lambiscón* and *barbero* (a flatterer of superiors), *igualado* (Aries, Self-assertion).
- **Beliefs and standing:** is obedience a virtue, a prudence or a weakness? Where authority comes from (God, nature, age, the people, expertise, ancestors, the land); the commandment to honor father and mother; Romans 13 on submission to rulers, and its rival readings; Catholic hierarchy and teaching authority against congregational polity; the Quakers' refusal of hat honor; Latter-day Saint priesthood authority; civil disobedience (Thoreau; King's "Letter from Birmingham Jail", 1963); leadership as service to the people, as Native nations state it; Lakoff's "strict father" and "nurturant parent" models (*Moral Politics*, 1996, Berkeley); when an order may be refused (the Nuremberg principle).
- **Feeling and the body:** deference in the body: lowered eyes, standing when someone enters, removing hats, kneeling, bowing, silence; eye contact read as respect in one tradition and as defiance in another (a classroom question in schools serving Native and Mexican-American children, to research, not assume); uniforms, robes, badges; voice, height and posture as cues of authority (studies of height and leadership *check*); fear and stress in police stops; deference to the doctor's white coat.
- **Everyday practice:** how children address parents and teachers; who decides in the household; first names or titles for bosses; speaking up to a manager; behavior at police stops; asking permission; whom people call when things go wrong (the police, the priest, the boss, the elders, the head of the family); ejido assemblies and the *comisariado ejidal*; homeowners' association boards; tribal councils and general meetings.
- **Life course:** parenting styles (Baumrind's authoritarian, authoritative and permissive, 1966-1971); whether parents want children to obey or to think for themselves (Kohn, *Class and Conformity*, 1969); teen rebellion; teachers' authority; the first boss; military service; gaining authority as a parent; elders' authority and its loss in frailty.
- **Ritual:** inaugurations and oaths of office (U.S. presidents and governors; the Mexican presidential sash, handed over on October 1 since 2024; British Columbia's lieutenant governor and the Speech from the Throne); *el Grito*, led by the president on September 15; the president's annual *informe*; royal visits and the 2023 coronation as seen from Canada; papal visits (Pope Francis's 2022 penitential visit to Canada); the installation of hereditary chiefs in the feast hall, as the nations describe it; change-of-command ceremonies; academic processions.
- **Institutions:** governments and their reach (the authoritarian-libertarian axis, above); police (the LAPD, the RCMP, Mexico's National Guard, municipal and tribal police); the military; courts; churches (the Archdiocese of Los Angeles, the largest in the U.S.; Latter-day Saint wards and stakes); schools; corporate org charts; unions; tribal governments, elected and hereditary; *usos y costumbres* municipalities; ombudsmen and inspectors general (British Columbia's Ombudsperson); civilian police oversight boards.

## 2. Duty

**Working definition** (settled under Capricorn by the author, 2026-11-02; wording proposed): what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.
**Facets:** duties to family (children, parents, kin); to the community and the nation (military service, jury duty, voting, taxes); to God and religious law; to the dead and to descendants; to one's work and office (professional duty, the duty of care); to the land, animals and waters; duties created by promises, vows and oaths; the state's duties to its people and to Native nations.
**Neighbors and opposites:** obligation, responsibility, loyalty, service, debt, vocation, commitment; shirking, neglect, desertion. **Excess:** dutifulness that crushes the self, blind loyalty, martyrdom, workaholism. **Deficiency:** shirking, freeloading, desertion.

- **Words:** "duty", "obligation", "it's my job", "do your part", "do your bit", "pull your weight", "duty calls", "above and beyond the call of duty", "civic duty", "jury duty", "on duty", "dutiful", "I owe it to them", "the least I can do"; Spanish *deber* (both "duty" and "must"), *obligación*, *cumplir* and *cumplido* (one who fulfills what is owed), *compromiso* (in Mexican usage also a social event one must attend), *quedar bien*, *el deber llama*; *tequio* (obligatory unpaid community work in Oaxacan towns, carried by migrants to Baja California and California), *faena*, *cargo* and *mayordomía* (rotating offices in Indigenous Mexican communities), *manda* (a vow to a saint, fulfilled by pilgrimage); the obligations of *compadrazgo*.
- **Beliefs and standing:** is duty the core of a good life or a burden to keep small? Duty against self-fulfillment; duties before rights or rights before duties; filial duty in Catholic, Protestant, Latter-day Saint and immigrant faiths; Kant's duty for duty's sake; the Stoic revival; *familismo* and its obligations (Sabogal et al., 1987); Native teachings that frame ties to land and kin as responsibilities more than rights, as the nations state them; Mexico's Constitution lists citizens' obligations (Articles 31 and 36, voting among them), while the U.S. Constitution and Canada's Charter list rights.
- **Feeling and the body:** the weight of obligation; guilt when it is unmet; caregiver stress and burnout; working while sick (presenteeism); bodily service (soldiers, nurses, firefighters).
- **Everyday practice:** caring for aging parents; sending remittances home; calling and visiting; attending weddings, funerals and baptisms (the *compromiso*); paying taxes; serving on juries; voting; volunteering; tithing; chores; serving in rotating community offices; keeping promises; mandated reporting of child abuse.
- **Life course:** chores and caring for siblings; whether the eldest child, or the eldest daughter, carries more (to research); the obligations a *quinceañera* or confirmation marks; Mexico's National Military Service for men at 18 (the *cartilla militar*); U.S. Selective Service registration; a first job's pay going to the household; marriage vows; parenthood; caring for parents; elders' duty to teach; duties to the dead (Día de Muertos altars; tending graves).
- **Ritual:** Memorial Day; Remembrance Day and the poppy in Canada; Mexican school flag ceremonies (*honores a la bandera* each Monday; *jura de bandera*; Flag Day, February 24); the Pledge of Allegiance in U.S. schools (California requires a daily patriotic exercise *check*); oaths of office, enlistment and citizenship; the juror's oath; Mother's Day (May 10 in Mexico) and Father's Day; pilgrimages to fulfill a *manda*; *tequio* days; the handing over of a *cargo*.
- **Institutions:** conscription and its history (Canada's 1917 and 1944 conscription crises; the U.S. draft and resistance to it in Berkeley); juries; taxes; compulsory schooling; the duty of care in tort law; fiduciary duty; the duty to warn (*Tarasoff v. Regents of the University of California*, 1976); duty-to-rescue laws (rare in U.S. states; Quebec's Charter; Mexico's offense of failing to aid, *omisión de auxilio* *check*); filial-responsibility laws (*check* which states); the Crown's duty to consult Native nations (*Haida Nation*, 2004) and the honor of the Crown; the U.S. federal trust responsibility to tribes; compulsory voting elsewhere (Australia), against Mexico's unenforced duty to vote; professional codes (the Hippocratic tradition; nursing pledges); military codes.
- **Rewards and sanctions:** medals and honors for service; praise as *muy cumplido*; guilt and shame; draft dodgers scorned or resisters praised, depending on the war; fines for skipping jury duty; family gossip about children who neglect parents; inheritance as the dutiful child's reward.
- **Expression:** "In Flanders Fields" (McCrae, 1915); the Mexican anthem's "a soldier in every son"; "we stand on guard for thee" in "O Canada"; *A Few Good Men* (1992; duty and unlawful orders); Ishiguro, *The Remains of the Day* (1989; duty at the cost of the self); *corridos* of soldiers and of sons who left to provide; proverbs (*el que obedece no se equivoca* *check*); obituaries that praise devotion.
