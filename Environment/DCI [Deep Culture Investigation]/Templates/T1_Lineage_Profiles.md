# DCI Stage 1: lineage profiles (field sets)

Eleven profiles per unit. Each becomes its own file in `Units/<unit>/01 - Lineage/`, named `NN - <Profile>.md`. Every file starts with a `STATUS:` line and ends with its sources. Every profile answers two questions: **what is it based on, and where did it come from?** Tag each claim (measured, official, scholarly, synthesis, classic, practitioner, claim). Fill **Variation inside the unit** in every profile.

## 01 - Family system
- Do married children live with or near their parents?
- Is inheritance split equally among children, or does one heir take most?
- Authority between parents and adult children.
- Where the unit sits in Emmanuel Todd's family types, if classified.
- Variation inside the unit.

## 02 - Kinship intensity
- Clans, lineages, extended-kin obligations.
- Cousin marriage: historical and present.
- How far loyalty to kin outranks rules and strangers.
- Ethnographic Atlas or kinship-intensity values for the unit's ancestral groups, if coded.
- Variation inside the unit.

## 03 - Religious lineage
- Traditions present, with shares (source, year).
- For each: branch, school and lineage; how it arrived.
- Folk, syncretic and vernacular layers.
- Secularization: how far, since when.
- Variation inside the unit.

## 04 - Subsistence
- What the founders and the earlier inhabitants lived by: herding, rice, wheat, plow farming, fishing, plantation, mining, frontier ranching, trade.
- Measured links that apply (honor culture and herding; rice and interdependence; frontier history and individualism; the plow and gender roles), with the evidence for this unit.
- Variation inside the unit.

## 05 - Land and inheritance
- How land was historically held: grants, homesteads, communal tenure, estates.
- Inheritance of land: one heir or partible.
- Present tenure.
- Indigenous land rights and settlements.
- Variation inside the unit.

## 06 - Political culture
- How authority, participation and the public good are understood.
- Elazar's classification (U.S. states) or the nearest equivalent elsewhere.
- Woodard's reading, where it applies.
- Variation inside the unit.

## 07 - Education
- Which school and university models it follows, and where they came from.
- What learning is for, as the culture states it.
- Variation inside the unit.

## 08 - Language
- Language family; languages spoken and their shares.
- Loanword layers: which outside languages supplied its words for money, religion, food, government.
- Colexification: concepts that share one word (from CLICS, where covered).
- Dialect and register differences.
- Variation inside the unit.

## 09 - Stories
- Tale types and myth motifs the unit's peoples carry (ATU, Thompson's *Motif-Index*, Berezkin's catalog).
- Founding stories and the heroes they hold up.
- Variation inside the unit.

## 10 - Song
- Song and music traditions, and what they say about social structure (Lomax's Cantometrics where coded).
- Variation inside the unit.

## 11 - Formative events
- The events the unit's people remember as making them: wars, migrations, famines, revolutions, colonization, disasters.
- Measured long-run effects, where studies exist (the persistence literature).
- Variation inside the unit.
