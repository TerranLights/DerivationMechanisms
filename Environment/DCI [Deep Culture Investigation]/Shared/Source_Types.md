# Source types: where a culture's understanding of a concept is recorded

**What this is.** The kinds of sources that hold evidence of how a culture understands its concepts, at the level of type, not title. Specific sources are chosen only when a particular culture is researched (author, 2026-11-10). Each concept's own source types are listed in its avenue file (`Shared/Avenues/`), under "Source types".

**How widely each type exists** matters most, because the method has to work for every culture. The types are grouped by where they are found:

| Tier | Where it exists | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Everywhere** | every human community, including those without writing, reached through recordings, collections and the people themselves | language, proverbs, stories, songs, games, ritual, names, objects, dress, buildings |
| **Writing** | wherever there is a written tradition | scriptures, sermons, letters, diaries, literature, dictionaries, histories |
| **The state** | wherever a modern state exists | school textbooks, census forms, money and stamps, honors and medals, national holidays, public speeches |
| **Mass media** | wherever there are print and broadcast media | newspapers, advertising, film, television, popular music |
| **Online** | wherever there is internet use | social media, forums, reviews, dating profiles, search data |
| **Outsiders** | nearly every culture, through those who came to it from outside | travelers, missionaries, colonial officials, ethnographers, culture guides for newcomers |
| **Scholarship and data** | wherever the culture has been studied or counted | ethnographies, theses, surveys, statistics, cross-cultural databases |

## 1. Language itself (everywhere; writing)
- **Everyday speech:** recorded conversations and spoken corpora (who interrupts, who praises themselves, how requests and refusals are made).
- **Dictionaries of every kind:** standard, historical, slang, dialect, children's, bilingual; the bilingual dictionaries missionaries compiled for hundreds of languages.
- **Grammars and language-teaching materials:** their example sentences and dialogues encode the culture's assumptions.
- **Phrasebooks for travelers:** what visitors are told they will need to say.
- **Translations:** how scriptures, novels and subtitles render the concept. Bible translations alone exist in thousands of languages; the word a translator chose shows the nearest local concept.
- **Loanwords:** a concept a language borrows a word for may have had no native one.
- **Proverbs, idioms and sayings.**
- **Names:** personal names and nicknames, place, street, business, team and pet names.
- **Forms of address and titles.**
- **Insults, slurs and swear words:** the worst thing one can call someone shows what is most despised.
- **Specialized slang:** of the military, trades, youth, sports.
- **Sign languages.**

## 2. Oral and folk tradition (everywhere)
- **Myths, legends and folktales,** with the tale-type and motif indexes that let them be compared across cultures.
- **Epics and praise poetry.**
- **Songs:** lullabies, work songs, laments, ballads, drinking songs, hymns, anthems.
- **Verbal contests:** riddles, verse duels, ritual insults, rap battles.
- **Children's games, rhymes and playground lore.**
- **Oaths, blessings, curses and prayers.**
- **Recited genealogies and histories.**
- **Gossip and rumor** (through ethnography).
- **Oral histories and life histories,** recorded and archived.

## 3. Ritual and the calendar (everywhere)
- **Life-cycle rites:** birth, naming, coming of age, marriage, retirement, funerals.
- **Festivals, holidays and the year's calendar.**
- **Pilgrimages and processions.**
- **Ceremonies of state:** inaugurations, oaths of office, national days, honors ceremonies.
- **Feasts and gift exchange.**
- **Healing rituals.**
- **Ritual speech:** toasts, eulogies, vows, oaths, acceptance speeches, farewells.
- **Sporting events** and their customs.

## 4. Things and places (everywhere)
- **Dress, ornament, tattoos, hairstyles,** uniforms and regalia.
- **Tools, weapons, toys.**
- **Houses and their layout; settlement plans.**
- **Shrines, home altars, graves and epitaphs.**
- **Monuments, statues and plaques; museum collections and labels.**
- **Signs, graffiti, murals.**
- **Vehicles and their decoration.**
- **Trophies, medals, certificates.**
- **Money, stamps, flags, seals, coats of arms, mottos:** every state chooses whom and what to honor on them.
- **Archaeological remains.**

## 5. Religion and morals (writing)
- **Scriptures and their commentaries.**
- **Sermons and homilies.**
- **Catechisms and religious-education materials.**
- **Confession and penance manuals:** lists of wrongs and how serious each is.
- **Lives of saints and martyrs.**
- **Religious rulings and guidance.**
- **Hymnals and prayer books.**
- **Votive offerings and *ex-votos*:** the painted thanks for a miracle, which record what people feared and asked for.
- **Ethical wills and conduct books.**

## 6. Advice and instruction (writing; mass media)
- **Etiquette manuals.**
- **Advice columns:** the letters show the dilemmas; the answers show the norm.
- **Parenting and child-care manuals.**
- **Self-help books.**
- **Marriage guides.**
- **Cookbooks and household manuals,** whose introductions state values.
- **Almanacs.**
- **Youth-organization handbooks:** Scouts and the like, with their badge requirements.
- **Coaching manuals.**
- **Military manuals and doctrine.**
- **Professional codes of ethics.**
- **Corporate codes of conduct, HR handbooks, training materials.**
- **Public-health and safety campaigns:** the state's view of what people should do.

## 7. Schooling (the state)
- **Textbooks:** readers, civics, history, literature.
- **Curricula and standards.**
- **National exams and essay prompts.**
- **School codes of conduct and dress codes.**
- **Report cards and teachers' comments.**
- **Awards and honor rolls; yearbooks and their superlatives.**
- **Graduation speeches.**
- **Children's literature.**

## 8. Literature and the arts (writing; mass media)
- **Literature of every genre.** Popular genres (romance, westerns, crime, comedy) state a culture's norms most plainly.
- **Poetry and drama.**
- **Comics and graphic novels.**
- **Film.**
- **Television:** soap operas and telenovelas, sitcoms, reality competitions.
- **Radio drama.**
- **Popular music and its lyrics; music videos.**
- **Visual arts and crafts; dance.**
- **Video games; fan fiction.**
- **Criticism and reviews:** how critics judge what is good.

## 9. News and media (mass media)
- **News stories.**
- **Editorials, opinion pieces, letters to the editor.**
- **Obituaries:** a community's final judgment of a life. Wedding and birth announcements.
- **Personal ads, classifieds and job ads.**
- **Advertising:** print, broadcast and outdoor ads, slogans and jingles.
- **Talk radio, call-in shows, talk shows.**
- **Press photography and documentaries.**

## 10. Online (online)
- **Social media:** posts, profiles and bios, hashtags, memes.
- **Forums and question-and-answer sites.**
- **Reviews** of products, services, places and employers.
- **Dating profiles.**
- **Videos and their comments; podcasts; blogs.**
- **Crowdfunding appeals and online petitions.**
- **Search data.**
- **Local-language online encyclopedias** and the arguments on their talk pages.
- **Online obituaries and memorial pages.**
- **Viral challenges.**

## 11. Personal documents (writing)
- **Diaries; letters and emails.**
- **Memoirs and autobiographies.**
- **Photo albums and home videos; scrapbooks.**
- **Autograph books and yearbook inscriptions.**
- **Wills.**
- **Family bibles and genealogies.**
- **Greeting cards:** the sentiments a culture sells.
- **Self-presentation genres:** résumés, cover letters, application essays.

## 12. Public records (the state)
- **Censuses and their forms:** the categories a form asks for show what the state thinks defines a person.
- **Statistics and vital records.**
- **Speeches of office.**
- **Citizenship study materials and oaths.**
- **Honor and award citations.**
- **Military and veterans' records.**
- **Commissions of inquiry and truth commissions.**
- **Public comments and complaint records.**
- **Immigration records.**
- **Colonial archives.**

## 13. Economic and commercial (the state; mass media)
- **Company materials:** histories, mission statements, annual reports.
- **Job descriptions and performance-review criteria.**
- **Packaging, catalogs, menus and price lists.**
- **Market research and consumer surveys.**
- **Real-estate listings.**
- **Insurance:** policies, claims, actuarial tables.
- **Patents.**
- **Business registries.**
- **Union contracts and newsletters.**
- **Trade journals.**
- **What people pay for:** sales of self-help, insurance, gym memberships, weapons, lottery tickets, as preferences revealed by spending.

## 14. Organizations (writing; the state)
- **The papers of clubs, churches, unions, associations and tribal councils:** minutes, bylaws, newsletters.
- **Charity appeals and annual reports; NGO reports.**
- **Records of mutual aid and communal work.**
- **Sports clubs' codes and histories.**
- **Histories of fire and rescue services.**

## 15. The people who handle the concept every day (the state; mass media)
Their trade literature, training manuals and professional forums describe how the local people behave.
- **Clinical case literature:** therapists, psychiatrists.
- **Social workers and counselors.**
- **Teachers.**
- **Military trainers.**
- **Clergy.**
- **Funeral directors, midwives, nurses.**
- **Interpreters and cultural mediators.**
- **Coaches, human-resources staff, insurers, emergency doctors.**

## 16. Insiders explaining themselves (writing to online)
- **National-character essays.**
- **Regional humor and comedy.**
- **"You know you're from here when..." lists.**
- **Local guidebooks written by locals.**
- **Identity quizzes; heritage organizations.**

## 17. Outsiders' records (outsiders)
- **Travel writing and guidebooks.**
- **Missionaries' letters and reports; colonial officials' reports.**
- **Explorers' journals; diplomats' memoirs; foreign correspondents.**
- **Guides for expatriates, business travelers and those facing culture shock.**
- **Orientation materials for immigrants and refugees.**
- **Military cultural handbooks.**
- **Guides for exchange students.**

## 18. Scholarship and data (scholarship and data)
- **Ethnographies and community studies.**
- **Local and national histories.**
- **Dissertations and theses:** every university produces them, often on its own region.
- **Studies in folklore, linguistics, cross-cultural psychology, sociology and economics.**
- **Cross-cultural databases:** the Human Relations Area Files; ethnographic atlases.
- **Surveys and polls; experiments; health and time-use surveys; administrative data.**
- **Encyclopedias,** general and regional.
