# Capricorn: the spirit of the law, moved here from the other signs

**What this file is.** Every law avenue the other ten signs held before law was taken out of them on 2026-11-10, moved here rather than deleted. DCI researches law only in Libra and Capricorn (author, 2026-11-10): **Libra takes the letter of the law** (what the law says, and how it is applied as written), **Capricorn takes the spirit of the law** (what it is for, and how people relate to it beyond its wording); the author notes the two "can often tend to be two different things". An item that bears on both appears in both files. Each item names the concept it came from and how it bears on it.

**To tidy when this material is used:**
- Some dates disagree between signs: *Mendez v. Westminster* (1946 or 1947); the potlatch ban (1884 or 1885 to 1951); California's paid family leave (2002 or 2004); Oregon's Black exclusion laws (dates vary); the Doukhobor exemption (1898 or 1899). Check each before use.
- Some items repeat between signs, or repeat what the home section already holds (`07 - Libra.md`, section 0; `10 - Capricorn.md`, section 8). Merge them when the material is used.
- Glosses given from memory carry *(check)*; two do not and should also be checked: the Boldt decision ("upheld treaty fishing rights in Washington") and the 1992 *ejido* reform ("lifted the bar on selling its land").

**Its home section:** `10 - Capricorn.md`, section 8, "The spirit of the law".

- **Dissolution (Rewards and sanctions):** the annulment route in Catholic families: church law used where divorce carries stigma.
- **Boundary (Rewards and sanctions):** legal sanctions for trespass.
- **Boundary (History):** Operation Gatekeeper (1994): the border enforced.
- **Escapism (Rewards and sanctions):** DUI arrests.
- **Escapism (Who it applies to):** prisoners (escape in the literal sense: Alcatraz, 1962).
- **Spirituality (Institutions):** chaplaincy in prisons.
- **Spirituality (Rewards and sanctions):** in the past, criminal penalties for Native ceremonies: the law used to punish a faith.
- **Surrender (Working definition):** the law, as one of the stronger things a person may surrender to.
- **Surrender (Facets):** turning oneself in.
- **Surrender (Words):** *entregarse*, to give oneself up to the police (also to God or a lover: one word for all three).
- **Surrender (Feeling and the body):** raised hands in arrest ("Hands up, don't shoot", 2014).
- **Surrender (Institutions):** the U.S. Marshals' Fugitive Safe Surrender (2005).
- **Surrender (Who it applies to):** prisoners.
- **Intuition (Everyday practice):** police instinct.
- **Intuition (Institutions):** algorithmic risk tools against judges' discretion (California's SB 10, 2018, overturned by Proposition 25, 2020): the official's judgment against the rule.
- **Intuition (Rewards and sanctions):** punishment when the hunch was prejudice.
- **Intuition (Exemplars):** detectives.
- **Intuition (Silences):** racial profiling.
- **Crime (Facets):** what the law calls crime and people don't, and the reverse: where the letter and the spirit part; crimes of the state and the police; punishment, restoration and redemption; the stigma of a record.
- **Crime (Neighbors and opposites):** lawfulness; overcriminalization (the excess); tolerated lawbreaking (the deficiency).
- **Crime (Words):** "felon", "offender", "inmate", "justice-involved person" (a newer person-first term); Spanish *delito* (the general legal term) against *crimen*; *falta* and *infracción*; terms in Native languages only as nations publish them (in their codes and law projects).
- **Crime (Beliefs and standing):** the rehabilitative ideal and "nothing works" (Martinson, 1974); "broken windows" (Wilson and Kelling, 1982); prison abolition (Critical Resistance, Berkeley, 1997; Ruth Wilson Gilmore, *Golden Gulag*, 2007); the Catholic Church's 2018 teaching that the death penalty is inadmissible: what punishment is for; Native laws as nations publish them (the Indigenous Law Research Unit at the University of Victoria; tribal codes); wrongs the people count as crime before the law did (wage theft, price gouging, elder abuse), and acts the law punishes that many don't count (jaywalking, file-sharing, small drug use).
- **Crime (Feeling and the body):** clothing profiled as criminal (zoot suits, 1943); the body under punishment (solitary confinement).
- **Crime (Everyday practice):** whether people report crime (ENVIPE asks why not: wasted time, distrust of authorities); tolerated petty crime (speeding, fare-dodging, tax cheating, bribes to traffic police).
- **Crime (Life course):** the school-to-prison pipeline; "scared straight" programs; aging prisoners; reentry.
- **Crime (Ritual):** the trial as a "degradation ceremony" (Garfinkel, 1956); sentencing; executions (once public); parole hearings; perp walks and mugshots; circle sentencing (*R v. Moses*, Yukon, 1992); the oral hearings of Mexico's reformed courts.
- **Crime (Institutions):** *reinserción social* (Mexico's 2008 constitutional term, replacing *readaptación*): what punishment is for, in the constitution's word; police and sheriffs; elected prosecutors (the recall of Chesa Boudin, 2022; the defeat of George Gascón, 2024): voters judging how hard the law is enforced; healing-to-wellness courts and banishment in tribal justice; Gladue reports: sentencing that weighs a Native offender's history; the correctional-control share.
- **Crime (Rewards and sanctions):** prison, fines, probation; record suspension and expungement: punishment and its lifting.
- **Crime (Exemplars):** the wrongly convicted (the National Registry of Exonerations): trust in courts tested.
- **Crime (History):** the Mexican Mafia, founded in a California prison (1957); arrests after the 1921 Village Island potlatch, and the U'mista Cultural Centre and the return of seized regalia: the law enforced against a ceremony, and the repair.
- **Crime (Measures):** FBI UCR and NIBRS (by agency); the Bureau of Justice Statistics; Statistics Canada's Uniform Crime Reporting Survey and Crime Severity Index (by census metropolitan area); its General Social Survey on Canadians' Safety; INEGI's ENVIPE (victimization, the *cifra negra*, trust in authorities); SESNSP crime incidence (municipal); GSS items on whether courts are harsh enough and on the death penalty; the World Justice Project's Mexico States Rule of Law Index.
- **Crime (Variation):** race and policing; immigration status (the "immigrant crime" question, answered from data); Native peoples' overrepresentation in prisons (reports of Canada's Correctional Investigator).
- **Crime (Relations):** law, order and authority.
- **Crime (Who it applies to):** police: whether the law reaches its own agents.
- **Crime (Silences):** crimes by police.
- **Crime (Change):** decriminalization and recriminalization (cannabis; drugs in Oregon and BC); restorative justice.
- **Fear (Institutions):** laws born of fear (Megan's Law; California's Jessica's Law, 2006; three strikes): punishment driven by fear.
- **Fear (Variation):** race and fear of police.
- **Fear (Who it applies to):** the police, as objects of fear.
- **Fear (Silences):** fear that is never reported; fear of police.
