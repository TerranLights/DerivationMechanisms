# The permission share: the activity list

STATUS: draft v0 (2026-11-10), for the author to settle. Not yet used for scoring.

**Measures the authoritarian-libertarian axis, which is interpreted in Capricorn's authority** (`Shared/Avenues/10 - Capricorn.md`, section 1; author, 2026-11-10). The numbers are collected at Stage 2; the scoring reads the law in force, as Libra's letter-of-the-law research does.

**What it is for.** The libertarian-authoritarian axis is measured the same way the author measures left and right (author, 2026-11-10): simple arithmetic on a fixed basis, giving the same answer whoever does the counting. The **permission share** measures how far the law reaches: of a fixed list of activities, what share does the law forbid or require permission for? Its companion, the **correctional-control share**, measures how hard the law is enforced (`Sources_and_Datasets.md`, section E). The list is the same for every jurisdiction in every target, so it is written to make sense anywhere in the world, not only in North America.

## How to score

**Each item is scored by the law in force at the place:**
- **Free = 0:** an adult may do it without asking anyone.
- **Permission = ½:** allowed only with a license, permit, registration, approval or waiting period specific to the act.
- **Forbidden = 1:** not allowed (a civil fine counts as forbidden), or allowed only in a narrow exception the item doesn't describe.

**Items marked ⚖ are the government's powers over a person.** They are scored in the same direction: 1 is the most power (it needs no one's approval), 0 the least (it is barred, or needs a judge's order based on evidence). Each item's note says what scores ½.

**The arithmetic.**
- **Domain score** = (items forbidden + ½ × items needing permission) ÷ items in the domain, as a percentage. This fills the libertarian/authoritarian column of each Part B row with a number.
- **Overall permission share** = the average of the eight domain scores. Averaging the domains, rather than pooling all items, keeps a domain with many items (personal conduct) from outweighing one with few.
- **0% is the far libertarian end** (nothing forbidden or licensed); **100% the far authoritarian end** (everything is).

**Rules that keep it repeatable:**
1. **Score the law as applied.** A suspended law (a moratorium, a statute blocked by a court) is read as not in force (author, 2026-11-10). A law in force counts whoever made it: the legislature, the voters or the courts.
2. **Two figures,** as for the spending measure: **own level,** only the items this government sets itself (the rest are left out of its count); **all levels,** every item, by the strictest law that applies at the place (a federal ban applies even where the state allows the act).
3. **Where local rules differ** inside the area (dry towns, zoning, short-term rentals), score the rule that covers the most residents and record the rest under Variation. County and city figures score their own local law.
4. **Score each item for an ordinary adult resident:** a citizen, of age, with no criminal record, unless the item says otherwise.
5. **A score says only whether the law forbids or licenses the act.** It makes no judgment about whether the law is right. Several items are contested: one side sees the law as protecting a right, the other as preventing a harm.
6. **The list is fixed once settled.** A change makes a new version (v1, v2), and every score already made is recalculated on it, so all scores always rest on the same list.

**Balance.** The last column says which side, in North American politics, more often restricts the act. It is used only to keep the list balanced, so that the measure doesn't turn back into left and right, and it isn't scored. Draft v0 has **13 items more often restricted by the left, 12 by the right, and 20 restricted by both or by state power whichever side governs.** The tags are Claude's reading; the author may change them.

## The list (draft v0, 45 items)

### 1. Economy: taxes, regulation, labor, welfare (4)
| # | Activity, as scored | Scoring note | Restricted more by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | A worker in a unionized workplace keeps the job without paying union dues or fees | 1 if the law lets the contract require dues or fees; 0 under a right-to-work rule | Left |
| 1.2 | A shop opens for business on Sunday | ½ if only some shops or hours may open; 1 if shops must close | Right |
| 1.3 | An adult sells homemade baked goods to the public from home | ½ if a permit or registration is needed; 1 if forbidden | Either |
| 1.4 | An adult drives passengers for pay in their own car, booked through an app | ½ if a driver's or company license specific to it is needed; 1 if forbidden | Either |

### 2. Property and land use: zoning, rent, eminent domain (4)
| # | Activity, as scored | Scoring note | Restricted more by |
