During the ULM and RWBEM processes, we research a variety and multitude of real-world locations as inspirational picks in order to develop in-universe settings. There's nothing inherently wrong with this. I'm not asking you to change it. What I'm saying is that this is an incomplete series of processes. At the moment, you're able to research real-world settings and locations, research what other things exist in those places, and use those as points of inspiration to establish what kinds of things are in particular Tepenian cities. This is fantastic, and it definitely needs to be part of the process. It just barely occurred to me that this folder is missing a currently-nonexistent yet critically-vital process that needs to be here, and that is:

*a systemized transmutation process for turning regular, "stock" locations/settings/items/etc into in-universe internally-consistent features*

So, for example, rather than "a bar like in [City-X]", instead, what *kind* of bar would get built by robots and humans working together in a robot-sovereign nation in a city in Antarctica. And I'm not only referring to the art that's on the walls (although that is definitely one part among many of the process). What would be really more to the point in this particular example is, what does it mean within a robot context for a place to be a "bar"? From the perspective of robots, what would it mean for a place to be a "garage"? From the perspective of robots, what would it mean for a place to be a "restaurant"? From the perspective of robots, what would it mean for something to be "graffiti"? From the perspective of robots, what would it mean for a place to be a "hospital"? Etc, etc, etc, and so on and so forth.

This process can only run after the ULM, DCI, CST, and the RWBEM are all fully complete. Plus, it will depend very, very, very heavily upon the results of the DCI, as well as the Robot Universals and Robot DCI work. At the moment, my tentative, working name for this new, yet-to-be-created process is the "Inspiration and Extrapolation to Setting Transmutation", or IEST.
